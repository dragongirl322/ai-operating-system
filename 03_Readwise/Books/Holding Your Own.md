## Highlights
- Use the following mental model when you’re mapping influence across your organization: • Inner circle: Direct boss, executive sponsors, decision-makers. • Middle circle: Cross-functional peers, collaborators, secondary approvers. • Outer circle: Legal, finance, HR, tech, PR, and anyone else who can delay or derail your plan if they’re caught off guard. People in your middle and outer circles are often overlooked, and that’s exactly where influence starts to break down. Think of your map like a radar. If someone’s outside the view, they can still take you down, so pull them into the process early. ([Location 1589](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B0FZWX57W5&location=1589))

- If you want a fast-track way to figure out what matters to your stakeholders, use the SCARF model, a neuroscience-backed framework developed by David Rock. SCARF stands for status, certainty, autonomy, relatedness, and fairness, which are five things our brains crave and react to when threatened. Each stakeholder is wired a little differently. Some want recognition, others want control, and still others want to feel included. Figure out what drives and triggers them, then tailor your approach accordingly. Influence gets a whole lot easier when you understand the psychology behind it. ([Location 1596](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B0FZWX57W5&location=1596))
