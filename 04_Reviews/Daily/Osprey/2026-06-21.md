## AI Signals

1. **AI governance became a G7-level operating problem, not a side policy track.** At the G7 meeting in Evian-les-Bains, France, AI CEOs and heads of state debated safe, rapid, effective AI deployment. AP reports that French President Emmanuel Macron and OpenAI CEO Sam Altman both called for international guardrails and testing forums. The timing matters because the discussion was overshadowed by a US directive that forced Anthropic to disable Fable 5 and Mythos 5 for all customers. Design implication: enterprise AI risk now includes model dependency, jurisdictional access, continuity planning, and the user experience of abrupt capability loss. For leaders, "trust" includes whether a product or workflow can keep working when governments or vendors change access.

2. **Public trust in AI companies remains thin even among a public that wants AI benefits.** Anthropic's Public Record survey, published June 12 from a late-2025 YouGov sample of 51,993 Americans, found strong hopes for disease cures and accessibility, but job loss, cognitive dependency, and misinformation were leading fears. Seventy-one percent backed government involvement in AI development and regulation, and only 15% said they trust AI companies to decide how AI is developed and used. This is a clean adoption signal: people do not just need better demos. They need accountability, liability, oversight, and experience cues that tell them who is responsible when AI harms, persuades, or degrades judgment.

## AI Design and Experience

3. **AI UX research is moving from interface usability to agent relationship design.** A June 17 arXiv paper, "Human-AI Agent Interaction in a Business Context," argues that as agents enter core business processes, teams need interaction patterns, UX criteria, measurement methods, and user-expectation research. This fits the shift you have been tracking: the designer is no longer just improving a screen; they are shaping delegation, trust calibration, decision authority, and handoff across human-AI work.

4. **AI is starting to evaluate UX, which creates both leverage and risk.** The June 4 arXiv paper "PerceptUI" proposes LLM agents as persona-conditioned synthetic users for UI/UX evaluation. Useful: faster early feedback, persona-specific rationale generation, and population-level response distributions. Risk: synthetic evaluators can harden model bias into design decisions if teams do not validate them against real user behavior. Design leaders should own when AI evaluation is acceptable, when human research is required, and what evidence is enough to ship.

## Human-Computer Paradigm Shifts

5. **The paradigm is moving from direct use to supervised delegation.** OpenAI's June 16 deployment-simulation work and the Axios-reported Google DeepMind AI Control Roadmap both point in the same direction: AI systems will act through tools, and safety work has to model behavior in realistic contexts, not just static tests. OpenAI is replaying realistic de-identified conversations and tool-heavy coding trajectories before release; DeepMind is reportedly treating future agents more like insider-threat systems that need layered monitoring. The HCI problem is situational awareness: what can the human see, question, reverse, and learn from when the AI is acting across systems?

## Design Leadership Problems

6. **The biggest organizational AI problem this morning is not access. It is coherence.** Business Insider's June 21 piece on "AI sprawl" cites Glean Work AI Institute survey data from 6,000 digital workers: many workers use multiple AI tools weekly and report individual time savings, but only 13% said those savings had significantly improved company performance. The recurring pain points are duplicate outputs, hidden "botsitting," weak shared standards, unclear ownership, and leaders rewarding usage instead of outcome quality. This is a design leadership problem because it is about operating model, not tool selection: define where AI belongs in the workflow, what good output looks like, who validates it, when work should stay human, and how teams share patterns instead of fragmenting into private AI workarounds.

## Why This Matters For You

This brief keeps landing in your trust-layer territory. The public conversation is no longer just "which model is best?" It is becoming "who can safely depend on whom, under what conditions, with what evidence, and with what recourse?" Your strongest lane is connecting the builder experience to the customer experience: governance that people can feel, safeguards that do not kill usefulness, and AI workflows that preserve agency while still delivering value.

The useful writing angle today: AI adoption is not a tooling race. It is a trust architecture problem. The organizations that win will design the relationship between human judgment, AI capability, oversight, and accountability before the absence of that design becomes visible as risk, cost, or non-adoption.
