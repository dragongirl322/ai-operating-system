# Soup Forks for 45 — Simple Brand Elements

## Brand idea

**Soup Forks for 45** is a tongue-in-cheek humor brand built around the idea that some beliefs require people to use the wrong tool for the job — like trying to eat soup with a fork.

The tone should be clever, playful, and slightly absurd. The brand should clearly signal political satire, but it should avoid sounding cruel, hateful, or personally demeaning. The humor works best when it points at flawed reasoning, misplaced confidence, contradiction, and willful blind spots rather than attacking people as inherently bad.

## Brand personality

- **Witty** — smart, quick, and layered.
- **Dry** — funny without over-explaining the joke.
- **Playful** — more eye-roll than outrage.
- **Clever** — rewards people who “get it.”
- **Lightly rebellious** — willing to poke the bear, but not interested in being nasty.
- **Observant** — notices contradictions and gently exposes them.

## Color guidance

Use **Cream of Soup** as the main canvas. It keeps the brand from feeling too aggressive and gives the satire a warmer, vintage feel.

Use **Patriot Navy** for structure and authority. It creates a subtle contrast between “official-looking” design and absurd messaging.

Use **Tomato Red** sparingly. It should feel like a wink, a stamp, or a punchline — not a wall of anger.

Use **Mustard Logic** for moments of emphasis, especially labels, stickers, callouts, or small visual jokes.

Avoid using pure red, white, and blue in equal amounts. That will make the brand feel too much like political merchandise. The goal is satire, not campaign branding.

## Typography

### Recommended logo / headline font

**Cooper Black**

Why it works: Cooper Black feels retro, funny, friendly, and slightly ridiculous in the best way. It makes the brand feel humorous before someone even reads the copy. It also softens the political edge.

Best uses:

- Logo
- Product names
- Short headlines
- Stickers
- Merch graphics

### Recommended secondary headline font

**Bebas Neue**

Why it works: Bebas Neue adds a bold, poster-like quality. It can make the brand feel punchy and public-facing without getting too formal.

Best uses:

- Subheads
- Labels
- Short slogans
- Social graphics
- “Official-looking” satirical statements

### Recommended body font

**Montserrat**

Why it works: Montserrat is clean, modern, readable, and neutral. It lets the joke land without competing with the headline font.

Best uses:

- Website copy
- Product descriptions
- Captions
- About page
- Longer explanations

### Font pairing rule

Use **Cooper Black** for humor and brand recognition. Use **Montserrat** for clarity. Use **Bebas Neue** only when you want something to feel louder, stamped, or poster-like.

A simple pairing:

- Logo: Cooper Black
- Headlines: Cooper Black or Bebas Neue
- Body: Montserrat Regular
- Buttons / Labels: Montserrat SemiBold or Bebas Neue

## Tone of voice

The brand voice should sound like a smart friend who sees the absurdity, raises an eyebrow, and makes one perfectly timed joke.

It should be:

- Clever, not cruel
- Dry, not angry
- Satirical, not preachy
- Playful, not childish
- Pointed, not hateful
- Confident, not smug

## Voice principles

### 1. Punch up at the idea, not down at the person

The target is faulty reasoning, contradiction, blind loyalty, and misplaced certainty — not someone’s intelligence, appearance, class, religion, or background.

Better:

> For moments when the facts were right there, but the fork was already in hand.

Avoid:

> Only idiots would buy this.

### 2. Let the joke breathe

Do not over-explain every reference. The name already asks people to think. The copy should do the same.

Better:

> A utensil for the truly committed.

Avoid:

> This is funny because soup should be eaten with a spoon, not a fork, which is like ignoring obvious facts.

### 3. Keep it light enough to wear in public

The best merchandise should feel witty enough for people to wear, gift, or post without feeling like they are starting a fight.

Better:

> Critical thinking sold separately.

Avoid:

> Your uncle ruined Thanksgiving again.

### 4. Use absurdity as the engine

Lean into kitchen, diner, utensil, soup, menu, recipe, and “wrong tool” metaphors.

Examples:

- Served with a side of cognitive dissonance.
- For when the spoon was too obvious.
- Ladle? Never met her.
- Now with 0% fact retention.
- A bold choice for a liquid-based meal.

### 5. Sound amused, not furious

The emotional register should be “I can’t believe this is real” rather than “I hate these people.”

Better:

> We checked the drawer. The spoon was right there.

Avoid:

> These people are destroying everything.

## Visual style

The visual style should feel like a mix of:

- Vintage diner menu
- Faux-official campaign merch
- Dry editorial cartoon
- Kitchen-table sarcasm
- Slightly absurd product packaging

Good visual motifs:

- Forks
- Soup bowls
- Ladles
- Diner checks
- Recipe cards
- “Official” seals
- Small stars or stripes used sparingly
- Faux warning labels
- Menu-board typography

Avoid:

- Mean caricatures
- Violent imagery
- Overly aggressive protest aesthetics
- Too much flag imagery
- Dark doom-and-gloom political visuals
- Anything that makes the brand feel hateful rather than humorous

## Design direction

The design should feel polished enough to be a real brand and silly enough that the joke lands immediately.

Think:

- Cream background
- Chunky retro headline
- Clean modern body copy
- Navy structure
- Red punchline accents
- Small fork or soup-bowl icon
- “Official utensil” style badges

## Brand guardrails

Do:

- Keep the humor smart and accessible.
- Make the joke about reasoning, contradiction, and absurd choices.
- Use metaphor and restraint.
- Let people feel clever for getting it.
- Make the brand giftable and wearable.

Do not:

- Use dehumanizing language.
- Mock protected traits or personal circumstances.
- Turn every message into a political rant.
- Over-explain the joke.
- Make the brand feel like hate merch.

## Overall brand feel

Soup Forks for 45 should feel like a wink across the table from someone who noticed the same thing you did.

It is political, but not heavy. It is pointed, but not cruel. It is funny because the metaphor is simple, the absurdity is obvious, and the spoon was right there the whole time.
