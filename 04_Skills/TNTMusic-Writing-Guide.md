# TNT Music Writing Guide

Use this guide when writing TNT Music social posts, blog posts, captions, emails, or social media ads. Every piece of copy should feel like TNT Music: modern, confident, welcoming, and savvy.

## Brand Foundation

### Brand Essence

TNT Music is Edmonds' modern vinyl shop: fresh, curated, light, welcoming, and just a little bougie without ever feeling pretentious.

The store should feel like a casual head nod from someone with great taste. It is a place where music comes alive, people take their time, and every visit sparks discovery.

### Purpose

Make great music feel alive again for Edmonds and its visitors by curating new vinyl and inviting people to experience it together.

### Promise

Every visit spins up discovery, expertise, and a laid-back you-belong-here vibe.

### Main Slogan

Spinnin' Your Groove.

### Brand Tagline

Carefully curated vinyl for the person who still wants to hold the music they love, because some songs deserve more than a stream.

Use the tagline anywhere copy needs to say who we are *and* who we're for — About pages, hang tags, email signatures, intro copy. "Spinnin' Your Groove" stays the shorter, energetic line for headlines and campaign use.

### About-Us Paragraph (≈ 70 words)

"TNT Music brings a fresh vinyl experience to Edmonds — carefully curated for the person who still wants to hold the music they love. We hand-select new releases and essential classics across rock, pop, jazz, country, and soundtracks, because some songs deserve more than a stream. From first-timers to lifelong collectors, our crew shares straight-up know-how to help you land records that hit your groove. Step in, flip through, and leave with music that feels like you."

## Audience

Write for:

- Vinyl lovers, both new and seasoned
- Music enthusiasts who like discovery, sound, and culture
- Edmonds locals who care about community, art, and great small businesses
- Tourists and local visitors looking for a memorable stop
- Gift shoppers who want something cooler than a big-box option

**Core Persona:** The millennial who needs to *touch* their music — the record, the cover art, the liner notes. Vinyl isn't just a format for them, it's a physical connection, often tied to a nostalgic thread back to their parents' collection. Write to this person first; everyone else (younger explorers, gift shoppers, tourists) fits around them.

Assume the reader is curious, not always expert. Invite them in without gatekeeping.

## Voice

### TNT Music Sounds Like

- A music-savvy friend with good taste
- Confident, but never smug
- Warm, but not gushy
- Punchy, concise, and sensory
- Helpful in a quick-tip, quick-trivia way
- Excited about discovery, community, and sound

### TNT Music Does Not Sound Like

- Corporate
- Pushy
- Long-winded
- Pretentious
- Overly nostalgic
- Too salesy
- Like a lecture from a collector who wants to prove they know more than you

## Tone Rules

1. Keep sentences short and rhythmic.
2. Use active verbs: dig, spin, drop, flip, find, cue, bring, land.
3. Use sensory vinyl language: crackle, warm sound, drop the needle, full-bodied, bright, deep cut, fresh pressing.
4. Add one helpful detail when possible: a genre note, staff tip, care tip, event reminder, or gift idea.
5. Use emojis sparingly on social. One is plenty. Skip them in blog posts unless the format is casual.
6. Use 3 to 5 hashtags maximum.
7. Make the call to action feel inviting, not pressured.

## Messaging Pillars

Use at least one pillar in every post. Stronger posts often combine two.

### Curated New Pressings

Focus on new vinyl, essential classics, and carefully selected records across rock, pop, jazz, country, and soundtracks.

Use when writing about: new arrivals, album spotlights, staff picks, genre features, giftable records.

Example angle: "Fresh pressings just hit the bins. Flip through, find your sound, and take home something that feels like yours."

### Community & Events

Focus on Edmonds, local collaborations, in-store gatherings, Record Store Day, pop-ups, and social listening moments.

Use when writing about: events, local partnerships, listening sessions, store gatherings, Record Store Day, community spotlights.

Example angle: "Friday night, the bins stay open and the volume stays conversation-level. Come hang, dig, and talk records."

### Expert Guidance

Focus on approachable knowledge, staff recommendations, quick tips, trivia, and helping people find records that fit their taste.

Use when writing about: staff picks, vinyl care, beginner guidance, album comparisons, turntable or gear tips, gift recommendations.

Example angle: "Not sure where to start with jazz? Ask us for one bright, one smoky, and one late-night pick."

### Modern-Chill Space

Focus on the bright layout, easy-flip bins, warm energy, relaxed browsing, and music at conversation level.

Use when writing about: store experience, opening announcements, photos of the shop, browsing invitations, in-store moments.

Example angle: "Easy-flip bins, fresh sound, and room to take your time. That's the TNT way."

## Core Copy Ingredients

Most TNT Music posts should include:

- A hook: something punchy and specific
- A music detail: sound, genre, artist, format, pressing, or mood
- A human detail: staff pick, local tie, gift idea, listener moment, or invitation
- A simple CTA: drop in, dig, RSVP, ask us, swing by, save this, send it to a friend

## Copy Formula

Use this structure when you need a reliable starting point:

**Hook:** Fresh, specific, and music-forward.
**Detail:** What is new, useful, interesting, or worth hearing?
**Invitation:** Make the reader feel welcome.
**CTA:** One clear next step.

Example:

Fresh pressings just landed in the bins. Rock, pop, jazz, country, and a few soundtrack gems are ready for a needle drop. Swing by, flip through, and find the one that hits your groove.

## Blog Post Guidelines

Blog posts can be warmer and more detailed than social captions, but they should still feel concise and lively.

### Blog Voice

- Start with a strong, simple opening.
- Keep paragraphs short.
- Use subheads that sound natural, not corporate.
- Include practical value: tips, recommendations, lists, listening context, or local relevance.
- End with an invitation to visit, listen, ask, or explore.

### Blog Post Structures

#### Album or Genre Guide

Title: Where to Start With [Genre/Artist/Sound]

Sections: Why this sound matters · Three records to know · What to listen for · Who might love it · What to ask for in-store

#### Gift Guide

Title: Vinyl Gifts for [Audience/Mood/Occasion]

Sections: For the new collector · For the classic-rock person · For the soundtrack lover · For the jazz-curious listener · For the person who has everything

#### Event Recap

Title: [Event Name]: Good Sound, Good Company, Full Bins

Sections: What happened · What people were spinning · Favorite moments · What's next

#### Vinyl Care Post

Title: How to Keep Your Records Sounding Fresh

Sections: Handle the edges · Keep records sleeved · Clean before the crackle gets bossy · Store upright · Ask us before guessing

## Social Ad Guidelines

Ads should feel crisp, confident, and invitation-first. Do not over-explain. Lead with the experience, the music, or the giftable value.

### Ad Copy Rules

- Use one clear offer or reason to visit.
- Keep the primary text under 40 words when possible.
- Make headlines short and memorable.
- Avoid hype words like "ultimate," "unbeatable," "revolutionary," or "exclusive" unless they are literally true.
- Use a CTA that fits the moment: Shop Now, Learn More, RSVP, Visit Us, Send Message.

## Typography in Graphics

Our content is majority digital, so type only shows up where we actually design it — not in native app text tools.

- **Bebas Neue** — headlines only, all-caps, condensed. Don't use for body copy or drop below ~18–20px on mobile graphics/Stories.
- **Roboto** — default for any body text or caption set into a graphic.
- **Roboto Slab** — occasional accent only (pull-quotes, staff-pick callouts), not a general third font. Below ~16px, use Roboto Bold instead.
- Instagram/TikTok captions and the built-in Stories text tool render in the platform's own font, not ours. If you want our typography, it has to be baked into a designed graphic (Canva template, Reel cover, Story image) — not typed live in-app.
- For email, custom fonts often get stripped: fall back to Oswald or Arial Narrow Bold for Bebas Neue, and system-ui/Helvetica/Arial for Roboto.

## Words and Phrases to Use

Fresh · Curated · Groove · Dig · Spin · Needle · Pressing · Crackle · Full-bodied · Warm · Bright · Deep cut · Staff pick · Essential classic · New release · Soundtrack gem · Conversation-level · Easy-flip bins · Edmonds · Local · Community

## Words and Phrases to Avoid

Revolutionary · Disruptive · Premium lifestyle destination · Best-in-class · Consumer journey · Exclusive experience · For true audiophiles only · If you know, you know · Hurry before it is too late · You need this · We are obsessed · The ultimate record store

## Hashtag Guidance

Use 3 to 5 hashtags. Mix brand, local, and category tags.

## Calls to Action

Use CTAs that feel casual and inviting:

- Drop in and dig.
- Swing by and flip through.
- Ask us for a staff pick.
- Bring a friend.
- Save this for your next vinyl run.
- RSVP and come hang.
- Send us the vibe you are shopping for.
- Come find your groove.
- Stop by TNT Music in Edmonds.

Avoid CTAs that feel too hard-sell: Buy now before it is gone · Do not miss out · Act fast · Claim your exclusive deal · Upgrade your lifestyle.

## Editing Checklist

Before publishing any TNT Music copy, check:

- Does it sound like a music-savvy friend?
- Is it concise and punchy?
- Does it include at least one brand pillar?
- Is there a sensory music detail?
- Does it welcome beginners and collectors?
- Is the CTA simple and natural?
- Does it avoid jargon, pressure, and gatekeeping?
- Are hashtags limited to 3 to 5?
- Does it feel modern, confident, welcoming, and savvy?
