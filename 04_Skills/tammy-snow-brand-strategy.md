## 01 · Brand Foundation

> I cultivate clear eyes, an open mind, and a tender heart so I can cut through the noise and create meaning, contentment, and joy — for myself and for others. With confident calm and authentic edge, I push boundaries to elevate people and organizations from strong to truly superb.

### Elevator Pitch

> I'm a design leader driven to fearlessly transform potential into outstanding performance. I combine clarity, compassion, and a healthy dose of badassery to drive innovation, inspire teams, and deliver undeniable results.

### Core Values

| Value | What It Looks Like in Practice |
|---|---|
| **Integrity** | Accountability in every action. Do the right thing even when it's hard. |
| **Curiosity** | Lifelong learner. Always looking for the next insight, connection, or challenge. |

3. **Strategic Clarity & Badass Accountability** — Cut through complexity with data, logic, and decisive action. Clear goals, real accountability, no noise. Get shit done.
4. **Authenticity & Individuality** — Don't perform leadership. Live it. Confident, calm, credible — with an edge that makes people lean in and creates psychological safety for others to be bold.

## 02 · Voice & Tone

Voice is consistent — it's who you are. Tone adapts to context — it's how you calibrate for the room.

### Voice Attributes

| Attribute | In Practice |
|---|---|
| **Precise** | No over-explaining. Cleanest path to the point. No jargon, no filler. |
| **Direct** | Say what you mean. Candor without cruelty. |
| **Grounded** | Calm authority. The room gets quieter when you speak. |
| **Edgy** | Say the thing others are thinking but won't say. |
| **Warm** | People feel seen. Real questions, real listening. |
| **Evidence-led** | Data, research, clear reasoning. Credibility over charisma. |

### Tone by Context

| Context | Tenor | How to Show Up |
|---|---|---|
| Executive peers | Calibrated, strategic | Peer-level. No hedging. Business implication first, data second. Challenge when you disagree. |
| Direct reports | Direct, warm | Clear expectations. Genuine acknowledgment. Candid feedback that shows belief in their potential. |
| Senior leadership | Precise, confident | Concise. Point first, context second. Bring the recommendation, not just the problem. |
| Customers & analysts | Credible, translating | Translate complexity into relevance. Outcomes, not methodology. |
| Industry / public | Thoughtful, edgy | Your perspective, not your company's. Provoke constructive thinking. Don't play it safe. |
| Written comms | Succinct, no jargon | Shorter than you think. One idea per paragraph. Active voice. |

### Language to Use

- Specific over vague: "We reduced review cycles by 40%" beats "we made real progress"
- Active over passive: "I decided" not "a decision was made"
- Direct questions: "What's your take?"
- Honest framing: "Here's what I know, here's what I'm working through"
- Strengths language: anchor feedback in what's working before naming what needs to change

### Language to Avoid

- Corporate filler: "synergy," "leverage," "circle back," "at the end of the day"
- Over-qualification: "I could be wrong, but..."
- Inflation: "I'm incredibly passionate about..." — passion is shown, not declared
- Vague superlatives: "world-class," "innovative," "best-in-class" without evidence
- Excessive hedging, upspeak, over-apologizing

## 03 · Visual Identity

Intersection of Mid-Century Modern structure, Scandi restraint, Japandi warmth, and industrial edge. Clean, considered, authoritative — warm enough to feel human, edged enough to feel distinct.

### Design Principles

- Restraint over decoration — every element earns its place
- Warmth within structure — materials and color create feeling; form creates credibility
- Honest materials — what you see is what it is
- Functional beauty — aesthetics serve purpose
- Considered negative space — silence is part of the composition

### Color System

Primary palette for 70% of any application; secondary and accents for 30% combined.

**Primary Palette**

| Color | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Slate Navy | `#1C2B3A` | Foundation / Authority |
| Steel Blue | `#4A7FA5` | Clarity / Calm |
| Warm Stone | `#F5F0EB` | Breath / Background |
| Charcoal | `#2D2D2D` | Body Type |

**Secondary Palette**

| Color | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Warm Amber | `#D4870E` | Energy / Edge / CTAs |
| Burnt Sienna | `#B85C38` | Heat / Emphasis (sparse) |
| Mid Gray | `#A09890` | Supporting Copy |
| Light Gray | `#E8E4DF` | Borders / Dividers |

**Color Usage Rules**

- Navy anchors every composition — primary background (dark mode) or dominant element
- Amber is the signature accent — CTAs, key data points, rule lines, emphasis. Never large areas
- Stone is the warm neutral — primary background for documents, slides, light mode
- Sienna sparingly — one instance per composition maximum. It signals "this matters most"
- Never more than three colors in a single composition. Navy + Stone + one accent is the default

### Typography

Structured, legible, unfussy. No decorative scripts. No mixed serif/sans pairings.

- **Primary face:** Inter or Aktiv Grotesk (Office default: Arial)
- **Display/Headlines:** Same face as body, heavy weight (700–900) at large sizes
- **Data:** Tabular figures, vertically aligned
- **Emphasis:** Weight + color, not italics alone. Bold + Amber for maximum emphasis. No underline (hyperlinks only)
- **Avoid:** Script fonts, serif/sans mixtures, all-caps beyond short labels, more than two weights per composition

**Type Scale**

| Level | Specification |
|---|---|
| Display | 40–52pt · Bold · Navy or White on dark |
| H1 | 28–32pt · Bold · Navy |
| H2 | 22–24pt · Bold · Navy or Charcoal |
| H3 | 18–20pt · SemiBold · Charcoal |
| Body | 11–12pt · Regular · Charcoal · 1.5 line height |
| Labels/Caps | 8–9pt · Bold · Amber · Letter-spaced 10–15% |
| Caption | 9–10pt · Regular · Mid Gray |

### Space & Layout

- Base grid: 8px multiples (digital); 0.125in increments (print)
- Margins: generous — 1in minimum for documents; 10–15% of page width for slides
- Paragraph spacing: 1.5x line height; visible space between sections
- Left-aligned body text; centered only for large display moments
- Industrial accent: single hairline rule (amber or charcoal) as structural device

### Imagery & Illustration

| Category | Direction |
|---|---|
| Photography | Candid, intentional. People in context, not posed. Natural light. Muted, film-adjacent tones. |
| Icons | Line icons only. Single weight. Never filled/solid in presentation contexts. |
| Data viz | Clean, minimal. Navy and amber as primary chart colors. Label directly; avoid legends. |
| Avoid | Stock photo aesthetics. Gradient overlays. Drop shadows on type. Anything that reads as "template." |

## 04 · Messaging Architecture

### Positioning Statement

> I'm a senior design executive who builds the organizational and human conditions for great work. I combine the precision of a researcher, the strategic instincts of an operator, and the conviction to push people and organizations beyond what they thought possible.

### Talking Points by Audience

**Senior Executives / C-Suite**

- "Design isn't decoration. It's how we reduce friction between users and the value we're trying to deliver."
- "My team's job is to translate ambiguity into direction."
- "I'll tell you what I know, what I'm figuring out, and what I need from you. That's the deal I want with you."

**Customers & Analysts**

- "We design from evidence, not assumption."
- "The measure of great design isn't how it looks — it's whether it reduces effort and increases clarity for the person using it."
- "I'd rather show you a rough prototype and have a real conversation than a polished deck that doesn't reflect reality."

**Industry Peers / Thought Leadership**

- "The design industry has a credibility problem. We've optimized for beautiful work that doesn't move the business."
- "The most powerful thing a design leader can do is make the case in business language — not design language."
- "Strong to superb is the standard. Superb is about what it enables, not how it looks."

**Team & Leadership Style**

- "My job is to remove the obstacles between talented people and great work."
- "I will be direct with you because I respect you. If I'm not being direct, that's when you should worry."
- "Accountability isn't a consequence — it's a covenant. We both have to honor it."

### What to Amplify

- **The research superpower** — a competitive differentiator in design leadership; use it more overtly externally
- **The turnaround narrative** — proven credibility stabilizing distressed teams
- **The "fierce + kind" combination** — bring it up and across, not just down and within
- **Translating design value into business language** — what executives and customers actually need

## 05 · Brand in Action

### Executive Presence

- Enter prepared. Know the business context before any senior conversation
- Point first. Recommendation, then rationale
- Pause before responding. Two seconds of silence signals thinking, not reacting
- Use the research training. Name the patterns others miss
- Let the edge show up the chain — bring "fierce and cool" to VP+ interactions

### Written Communications

- **Weekly team email:** maintain the cadence. Succinct, forward-looking
- **Exec updates:** bottom-line up front. Three bullets max for status; one clear ask
- **LinkedIn / thought leadership:** your POV, not your company's. Provoke thinking. Short is more shareable
- **Proposals / strategy docs:** structure is the argument. Clear headings, clear recommendations, evidence-backed

### Behavioral Anchors

| Behavior | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| **Do the right thing** | Even when unpopular or uncomfortable. Named as a superpower — protect it. |
| **Close the loop** | Build the habit of explicit closing across multi-conversation thinking. |
| **Delegate with confidence** | You built the team. Trust them. Full scope requires releasing the details. |
| **Evangelize upward** | Make the work visible at the level above. That's the gap — fix it. |
| **Protect your calendar** | Deep work and strategic relationships require protected space. |
| **Amplify impact** | Narrating value clearly is a leadership responsibility, not self-promotion. |

## 07 · Quick Reference

### Brand in Three Sentences

> I'm a design executive who transforms potential into performance — for teams, products, and organizations. I lead with evidence, act with courage, and hold people to standards that make strong work genuinely superb. Clear eyes, open mind, tender heart — and the guts to say what needs to be said.

### The Five Things People Can Always Count On

1. You will do the right thing, even when it costs you something
2. You will tell them the truth — directly, without cruelty
3. You will listen before you speak, and when you speak it will matter
4. You will hold the standard without making people feel small for not meeting it yet
5. You will show up as yourself — no performance, no mask, no corporate veneer

|---|---|---|

- No script fonts. No decorative faces. No serif/sans mixtures
- Hierarchy through weight and size, not typeface variety

### The Five Questions Before You Communicate

1. What's the one thing I want them to walk away knowing?
2. Am I leading with the point, or burying it?
3. Is there a word I can cut without losing meaning?
4. Am I hedging? Why?
5. Does this sound like me, or like what I think I should sound like?
