## 01 · Brand Foundation

### Who You Are

This document is your strategic anchor. It defines how you show up, what you stand for, and how that translates into every interaction, whether you're in the room with a C-suite executive, speaking at an industry event, or building your next chapter.

### Brand Essence

> I cultivate clear eyes, an open mind, and a tender heart so I can cut through the noise and create meaning, contentment, and joy, for myself and for others. With confident calm and authentic edge, I push boundaries to elevate people and organizations from strong to truly superb.

### Elevator Pitch

> I'm a design leader driven to fearlessly transform potential into outstanding performance. I combine clarity, compassion, and a healthy dose of badassery to drive innovation, inspire teams, and deliver undeniable results.

### Core Values

|Value|What It Looks Like in Practice|
|---|---|
|**Integrity**|Accountability in every action. You do the right thing even when it's hard.|
|**Curiosity**|Lifelong learner. Always looking for the next insight, connection, or challenge.|
|**Compassion**|Genuine care for people's experiences without sacrificing honesty or standards.|
|**Excellence**|Strong to superb is the standard. You raise the bar and expect others to meet you there.|
|**Family First**|The value that grounds all others. Personal well-being is not a trade-off; it's the foundation.|

### Brand Pillars

**1 · Elevating Excellence** You don't do 'good enough.' You take what's already strong and make it superb, whether that's a design system, a team, or a process. This isn't perfectionism; it's standards-based leadership.

**2 · Empowering Through Kindness & Courage** Genuine care is not softness. It's the prerequisite for trust, and trust is the prerequisite for high performance. You hold both, warmth and the willingness to say the hard thing.

**3 · Strategic Clarity & Badass Accountability** You cut through complexity with data, logic, and decisive action. You set clear goals, hold people (including yourself) accountable, and eliminate noise. You get shit done.

**4 · Authenticity & Individuality** You don't perform leadership. You live it. Confident, calm, credible, with an edge that makes people lean in. That authenticity creates psychological safety for others to be bold too.

## 02 · Voice & Tone

### How You Sound

Voice is consistent, it's who you are. Tone adapts to context, it's how you calibrate for the room. Both should feel like Tammy Snow, not a corporate press release or a motivational poster.

### Voice Attributes

|Attribute|What It Means in Practice|
|---|---|
|**Precise**|You don't over-explain. You find the cleanest path to the point and take it. No jargon, no filler.|
|**Direct**|You say what you mean. Candor without cruelty, honest feedback delivered with care.|
|**Grounded**|Calm authority. The room gets quieter when you speak, not louder.|
|**Edgy**|You're willing to say the thing others are thinking but won't say. That's what makes you memorable.|
|**Warm**|People feel seen in your presence. You ask real questions and actually listen to the answers.|
|**Evidence-led**|You back your positions with data, research, and clear reasoning. Credibility over charisma.|

### Tone by Context

|Context|Tenor|How to Show Up|
|---|---|---|
|**Executive peers**|Calibrated, strategic|Peer-level. No hedging. Lead with the business implication, support with the data. Challenge when you disagree.|
|**Direct reports**|Direct, warm|Clear expectations. Genuine acknowledgment. Candid feedback that shows you believe in their potential.|
|**Senior leadership**|Precise, confident|Concise. Point first, context second. Bring the recommendation, not just the problem.|
|**Customers & analysts**|Credible, translating|Translate complexity into relevance. What does this mean for them? Lead with outcomes, not methodology.|
|**Industry / public**|Thoughtful, edgy|Your perspective, not your company's. Provoke constructive thinking. Don't play it safe.|
|**Written comms**|Succinct, no jargon|Shorter than you think. One idea per paragraph. Active voice. The weekly team email is a model to maintain.|

### Language to Use

- Specific over vague: 'We reduced review cycles by 40%' beats 'we made real progress'
- Active over passive: 'I decided' not 'a decision was made'
- Direct questions: 'What's your take?' not 'I was wondering if you might have thoughts'
- Honest framing: 'Here's what I know, here's what I'm working through', intellectual honesty builds trust
- Strengths language: anchor feedback in what's working before naming what needs to change

### Language to Avoid

- Corporate filler: 'synergy,' 'leverage,' 'circle back,' 'at the end of the day'
- Over-qualification: 'I could be wrong, but...' or 'This might not be the right idea...'
- Inflation: 'I'm incredibly passionate about...', passion is shown, not declared
- Vague superlatives: 'world-class,' 'innovative,' 'best-in-class' without evidence
- Excessive hedging: making every statement a question via upspeak or over-apologizing

## 03 · Visual Identity

### How You Look

Your visual identity draws from the intersection of Mid-Century Modern structure, Scandi restraint, Japandi warmth, and industrial edge. The result: clean, considered, authoritative, with enough warmth to feel human and enough edge to feel distinct.

### Design Principles

- Restraint over decoration, every element earns its place
- Warmth within structure, materials and color create feeling; form creates credibility
- Honest materials, what you see is what it is; no artifice
- Functional beauty, aesthetics serve purpose, not the other way around
- Considered negative space, silence is part of the composition

### Color System

Primary palette is warm and grounding. Secondary and accents add energy and emphasis. Use primaries for 70% of any application, secondary and accents for 30% combined.

**Primary Palette**

|Hex|Color|Role|
|---|---|---|
|`#2E4A3C`|Forest Pine|Foundation / Authority|
|`#948873`|Warm Taupe|Clarity / Calm|
|`#F5F0EB`|Warm Stone|Breath / Background|
|`#2E2A25`|Warm Charcoal|Body Type|

**Secondary Palette**

|Hex|Color|Role|
|---|---|---|
|`#BE7355`|Terracotta|Energy / Edge / CTAs|
|`#A84E30`|Deep Sienna|Heat / Emphasis (sparse)|
|`#ADA595`|Warm Greige|Supporting Copy|
|`#E7DFD3`|Soft Sand|Borders / Dividers|

### Color Usage Rules

- Forest Pine anchors every composition. Use as primary background (dark mode) or dominant element.
- Terracotta is the signature accent. Use for calls to action, key data points, rule lines, and moments of emphasis. Not for large areas.
- Stone is the warm neutral. Primary background for documents, slides, and light-mode applications.
- Sienna is used sparingly. One instance per composition maximum. It signals 'this matters most.'
- Never use more than three colors in a single composition. Pine + Stone + one accent is the default.

### Typography

Typography carries the warmth. Optima is humanist and calligraphic, with subtly flared strokes that feel considered and human without going soft. One face does all the work, so hierarchy comes from weight and size, not variety. No decorative scripts. Clean hierarchies.

|Role|Specification|
|---|---|
|**Primary Face**|Optima, humanist face with flared strokes. Elegant, warm, grounded. Native on macOS. Substitutes: URW Classico (licensed clone), Candara (Office and Windows).|
|**Display / Headlines**|Same face as body, set in bold at large sizes. Restraint is the statement.|
|**Data & Numbers**|Tabular figures for any data presentation. Numbers should align vertically.|
|**Emphasis**|Weight and color, not italics alone. Bold + Terracotta for maximum emphasis. Avoid underline (reserved for hyperlinks).|
|**Avoid**|Decorative script of any kind. All-caps beyond short labels. More than two weights per composition.|

**Type Scale (Document / Slide Use)**

|Level|Specification|
|---|---|
|**Display**|40–52pt · Bold · Pine or White on dark|
|**H1**|28–32pt · Bold · Pine|
|**H2**|22–24pt · Bold · Pine or Charcoal|
|**H3**|18–20pt · SemiBold · Charcoal|
|**Body**|11–12pt · Regular · Charcoal · 1.5 line height|
|**Labels/Caps**|8–9pt · Bold · Terracotta · Letter-spaced 10–15%|
|**Caption**|9–10pt · Regular · Warm Greige|

### Space & Layout

Japandi and Scandi influence shows in how space is used. The grid is tight, the margins are generous, and every element has room to breathe. Density is not sophistication.

- Base grid: 8px or multiples thereof for digital; 0.125in increments for print
- Margins: generous, documents at 1in minimum; slides at 10–15% of page width
- Paragraph spacing: 1.5x line height; visible space between sections
- Alignment: left-aligned for body text; centered only for large display moments
- Industrial accent: occasional use of a single hairline rule (terracotta or charcoal) as a structural device

### Imagery & Illustration Direction

|Category|Direction|
|---|---|
|**Photography**|Candid, intentional. People in context, not posed. Natural light. Muted, film-adjacent tones that align with the stone and pine palette.|
|**Icons**|Line icons only. Single weight. Never filled/solid icons in presentation contexts.|
|**Data viz**|Clean, minimal. Pine and terracotta as primary chart colors. Label directly; avoid legends when possible.|
|**Avoid**|Stock photo aesthetics. Gradient overlays. Drop shadows on type. Anything that reads as 'template.'|

## 04 · Messaging Architecture

### What You Say

Consistent messaging across contexts reinforces credibility. These are not scripts, they're anchors. Know them well enough that they come out naturally.

### Positioning Statement

> I'm a senior design executive who builds the organizational and human conditions for great work. I combine the precision of a researcher, the strategic instincts of an operator, and the conviction to push people and organizations beyond what they thought possible.

### Talking Points by Audience

**With Senior Executives / C-Suite**

- 'Design isn't decoration. It's how we reduce friction between users and the value we're trying to deliver.'
- 'My team's job is to translate ambiguity into direction. Here's how we're doing that on [initiative].'
- 'I'll tell you what I know, what I'm figuring out, and what I need from you. That's the deal I want with you.'

**With Customers & Analysts**

- 'We design from evidence, not assumption. Here's what the research tells us about [problem space].'
- 'The measure of great design isn't how it looks, it's whether it reduces effort and increases clarity for the person using it.'
- 'I'd rather show you a rough prototype and have a real conversation than a polished deck that doesn't reflect reality.'

**With Industry Peers / Thought Leadership**

- 'The design industry has a credibility problem. We've optimized for beautiful work that doesn't move the business. That has to change.'
- 'The most powerful thing a design leader can do is make the case in business language, not design language.'
- 'Strong to superb is the standard I hold myself to. Not perfect. Superb is about what it enables, not how it looks.'

**On Your Team & Leadership Style**

- 'My job is to remove the obstacles between talented people and great work.'
- 'I will be direct with you because I respect you. If I'm not being direct, that's when you should worry.'
- 'Accountability isn't a consequence, it's a covenant. We both have to honor it.'

### What to Amplify

Based on your 360 feedback and brand positioning, these are the themes to lean into more publicly:

- The research superpower, it's a competitive differentiator in design leadership; use it more overtly in external conversations
- The turnaround narrative, you've built credibility stabilizing distressed teams; that's a proven leadership story
- The 'fierce + kind' combination, the edgier side of your brand shows up down and within your team; bring it up and across
- Translating design value into business language, this is what executives and customers actually need from a design leader

## 05 · Brand in Action

### How You Operate

### Executive Presence

Presence is not volume. It's the quality of attention you command when you walk in and when you speak. Your 360 scores you at the top of the room on integrity, compassion, and courage. These are your presence anchors.

- Enter prepared. Know the business context before any conversation with senior leadership.
- Point first. State your recommendation, then your rationale. Not the other way around.
- Pause before responding. The two seconds of silence signals you're thinking, not reacting.
- Use your research training. You see patterns others miss, name them. That's the superpower.
- Let your edge show up the chain. The 'fierce and cool' reads clearly within your team. Bring it to VP+ interactions too.

### Written Communications

- Weekly team email: maintain the cadence. It's a noted differentiator. Keep it succinct and forward-looking.
- Exec updates: bottom-line up front. Three bullets max for status; one clear ask.
- LinkedIn / thought leadership: your point of view, not your company's. Provoke thinking. Short is more shareable than long.
- Proposals and strategy docs: structure is the argument. Clear headings, clear recommendations, evidence-backed.

### Behavioral Anchors

Your brand lives in behavior more than words. These are the non-negotiables:

|Behavior|Why It Matters|
|---|---|
|**Do the right thing**|Even when it's unpopular. Even when it's uncomfortable. Your 360 names this as a superpower, protect it.|
|**Close the loop**|You evolve your thinking through multiple conversations without always looping back. Build the habit of explicit closing.|
|**Delegate with confidence**|You've built the team. Trust them. Bandwidth for your full scope requires releasing the details.|
|**Evangelize upward**|Your work and your team's work isn't visible enough at the level above Jeff. That's the gap. Fix it.|
|**Protect your calendar**|Others are managing your time. Reclaim it. Deep work and strategic relationships require protected space.|
|**Amplify impact**|Not self-promotion. Narrating value clearly is a leadership responsibility. 'Here's what we built. Here's what it changed.'|

- **THESIS:** Great design leadership is organizational leadership. The work is building the conditions, not doing the work yourself
- **FORWARD:** Building the playbook for design leaders who want to operate at the level of the business, not alongside it
