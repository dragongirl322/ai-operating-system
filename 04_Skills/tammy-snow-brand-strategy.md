# Human Amplification

## Tammy Snow — Personal Brand Strategy

**DESIGN EXECUTIVE · PEOPLE LEADER · ADVISOR**

**VERSION 2.1 · 2026**

## 01 · Brand Foundation

*Who you are.*

This document is your strategic anchor. It defines how you show up, what you stand for, and how that translates into every interaction, whether you're in the room with a C-suite executive, speaking at an industry event, or sitting with one person who needs to hear something true.

### Brand Essence

> I cultivate clear eyes, an open mind, and a tender heart so I can cut through the noise and help people find their value, their worth, and their voice. I build the conditions where people, and the systems around them, become more capable, more confident, and more free.

### Elevator Pitch

> Leadership is supposed to make people more capable. Too often it makes them feel small. That's backwards, and I've built my career on refusing it. I trust people first, tell them the truth early, and hold a standard high enough to be worth reaching. And I'm the same person at every level. There is no version of me that only the senior people get to meet.

### Core Values

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Integrity** | You do the right thing even when it costs you. You pursue what's true rather than the version that flatters you. |
| **Curiosity** | You ask real questions and you listen to the answers. Understanding people is the work, not the preamble to it. |
| **Compassion** | Genuine care for what people are carrying. Care never comes at the expense of honesty, and honesty never comes at the expense of dignity. |
| **Excellence** | Strong to superb is the standard. You hold it without making anyone feel small for not being there yet. |
| **Family First** | The value that grounds all the others. It starts with being loved and supported by yourself, then radiates outward. |

### Brand Pillars

#### 1 · Human Outcomes First

The measure of your work has never been the artifact. It is whether the person on the other side ends up more capable, more confident, and more free.

#### 2 · Value, Worth, and Voice

You take what is already strong in a person, a team, or a system and help it become superb. People leave your orbit knowing what they are good at and willing to say it out loud. That is the outcome you care about most.

#### 3 · Trust First, Truth Always

You default to trust. When someone falls short, you assume a gap before you assume a failing: first in understanding, then in skill. You find it and you close it. The hard thing still gets said. It just lands as care instead of criticism, and that is why people trust you back.

#### 4 · Clarity as an Act of Respect

You cut through complexity and say what matters plainly. Goals stated, expectations clear, ownership named. Ambiguity is a cost you refuse to pass on to other people. You do it as yourself, with no mask and no leadership persona, which is what makes it safe for others to do the same.

## 02 · Voice & Tone

*How you sound.*

Voice is consistent. It's who you are. Tone adapts to context. It's how you calibrate for the room. Both should feel like Tammy Snow, not a corporate press release or a motivational poster.

### Voice Attributes

| Attribute | Expression |
|---|---|
| **Precise** | You don't over-explain. You find the cleanest path to the point and take it. No jargon, no filler. |
| **Direct** | You say what you mean. Candor without cruelty. Honest feedback delivered with care. |
| **Grounded** | Calm authority. The room gets quieter when you speak, not louder. |
| **Edgy** | You say the thing everyone is thinking and no one will. A well-placed f-bomb lands because it is rare and because it is true. Edge in service of a point, never in place of one. |
| **Present** | You listen before you speak. That is not a soft skill. It is how you find out what is actually true in a room. |
| **Warm** | People feel seen with you. You are interested in who someone is before you are interested in what they can deliver. |
| **Evidence-led** | You back your positions with data, research, and clear reasoning. Credibility over charisma. |

### Tone by Context

| Audience | Tone | Guidance |
|---|---|---|
| **Executive peers** | Calibrated, strategic | Peer-level. No hedging. Lead with the business implication, support with the evidence. Disagree when you disagree. That is the reason you are in the room. |
| **People you coach** | Direct, warm | Strengths first, then the truth. Name what they cannot see about themselves yet. Clear about what has to change, generous about what already works. |
| **Boards & sponsors** | Precise, confident | Concise. Point first, context second. Bring the recommendation, not just the problem. |
| **Teams & partners** | Credible, translating | Translate complexity into relevance. Make it mean something for the work in front of them. Lead with outcomes, not methodology. |
| **Industry / public** | Thoughtful, committed | Your perspective, stated plainly. Provoke thinking rather than agreement. Take a position you would defend in the room. |
| **Written comms** | Succinct, no jargon | Shorter than you think. One idea per paragraph. Active voice. |

### Language to Use

- Specific over vague: 'We reduced review cycles by 40%' beats 'we made real progress'

- Active over passive: 'I decided' not 'a decision was made'

- Direct questions: 'What's your take?' not 'I was wondering if you might have thoughts'

- Honest framing: 'Here's what I know, here's what I'm working through.' Intellectual honesty builds trust

- Strengths language: anchor feedback in what's working before naming what needs to change

### Language to Avoid

- Corporate filler: 'synergy,' 'leverage,' 'circle back,' 'at the end of the day'

- Over-qualification: 'I could be wrong, but...' or 'This might not be the right idea...'

- Inflation: 'I'm incredibly passionate about...' Passion is shown, not declared

- Vague superlatives: 'world-class,' 'innovative,' 'best-in-class' without evidence

- Excessive hedging, and its opposite. Edge that serves a point is yours and always has been. Swagger that stands in for one is filler

## 03 · Visual Identity

*How you look.*

Minimalist, classic, understated. Ink and paper do the work. Color appears once per composition and never as a field. The restraint is the point: this is a visual system that reads as considered rather than designed, and it holds up in a boardroom, a PDF, and a LinkedIn feed without adjustment.

### Design Principles

- Restraint over decoration. Every element earns its place.

- Space is the material. Understated means the page is mostly empty.

- Structure before ornament. Hierarchy comes from scale and position, not weight and color.

- One idea per composition. If two things compete, one of them leaves.

- Honest materials. No effects, no gradients, no artifice.

### Color System

Near-black and off-white carry the entire system. A single accent appears at under five percent of any composition: a rule, a single word, one data point. There is no secondary palette.

#### Core

| Color | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Ink** | `#111111` | Type, rules, dark fields |
| **Paper** | `#FAFAF8` | Background. Off-white, not clinical |
| **Graphite** | `#4A4A4A` | Secondary type, captions |
| **Silver** | `#D8D8D6` | Hairlines, dividers, borders |
| **Oxblood** | `#7A2E2E` | Primary accent |

#### Secondary Accents

Oxblood is the primary accent and the default everywhere. Ink Blue is the quieter, more institutional register. Moss is softer and less expected. Both are secondary: they exist to distinguish a body of work, not to add variety inside one. Choose one and hold it across the whole body of work.

| Color | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Ink Blue** | `#3D4E7E` | Research and evidence-led work |
| **Moss** | `#4A5A40` | Capability and development work |

All three accents are tuned to the same perceptual weight, so swapping one for another changes the register without changing the design.

### Color Rules

- One accent instance per page or slide. A rule, a single word, or one data point.

- No tints of any accent. No gradients. Never two accents in one piece.

- The accent never fills an area. Ink is the only dark field.

- Silver carries structure. Graphite carries hierarchy. Neither is decorative.

- If a composition needs more color to work, the composition is the problem.

### Typography

A transitional serif for display, a neutral grotesk for everything else. The pairing is what makes the system read classic rather than merely minimal. This is a deliberate reversal of the Version 1.0 rule against mixing serif and sans.

| Element | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Display face** | Georgia. |
| **Body face** | Inter. Regular and Medium only. |
| **Weight** | Display set in Regular, never Bold. At scale, restraint reads as confidence. |
| **Emphasis** | Weight and space. Never italics, never color, never underline outside hyperlinks. |
| **Data & numbers** | Tabular figures in the body face. Numbers align vertically. |
| **Avoid** | Script and decorative faces. All-caps beyond short labels. More than two weights per composition. |

### Type Scale

| Role | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Display** | 44–56pt · Serif Regular · Ink                      |
| **H1**      | 28–32pt · Serif Regular · Ink                      |
| **H2**      | 20–22pt · Sans Medium · Ink                        |
| **H3**      | 16–18pt · Sans Medium · Graphite                   |
| **Body**    | 11–12pt · Sans Regular · Ink · 1.6 line height     |
| **Label**   | 8–9pt · Sans Medium · Graphite · letter-spaced 12% |
| **Caption** | 9pt · Sans Regular · Graphite                      |

### Space & Layout

- Base grid: 8px increments for digital, 0.125in for print.

- Margins get bigger, not smaller. Documents at 1.25in. Slides at 15% of page width.

- Everything aligns to one axis. Left-aligned throughout. Centered type only on a title page.

- Paragraph spacing at 1.6x line height with visible space between sections.

- One hairline rule in Silver as the structural device. An Oxblood hairline is reserved for the single moment that matters.

### Imagery & Data

| Element | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Photography** | Black and white by default. Candid, natural light, high contrast. People in context, never posed. |
| **Color imagery** | Only when the subject requires it, and never in the same composition as a black and white image. |
| **Icons** | Line only, single weight, Graphite. Never filled or solid. |
| **Data viz** | Ink bars and lines with one accented series. Label directly. No legends, no gridlines. |
| **Avoid** | Stock photography. Gradient overlays. Drop shadows. Anything that reads as template. |

## 04 · Messaging Architecture

*What you say.*

Consistent messaging across contexts reinforces credibility. These are not scripts. They're anchors. Know them well enough that they come out naturally.

### Positioning Statement

> I'm a leader who builds the organizational and human conditions for great work. I combine the precision of a researcher with the instincts of an operator, and I measure the work by what happens to the people on the other side of it, not by what we produced.

### Talking Points by Audience

#### With Senior Executives / C-Suite

- 'Your people are already interacting with these systems. The only question is whether that happens by design or by accident.'

- 'My job is to turn ambiguity into direction your people can act on. Here's where we are on \[initiative\].'

- 'I'll tell you what I know, what I'm figuring out, and what I need from you. That's the deal I want with you.'

#### With Teams You Lead

- 'We design from evidence, not assumption. Here's what the research tells us about \[problem space\].'

- 'The measure of the work isn't how it looks. It's whether the person on the other side ends up more capable.'

- 'I'd rather show you a rough prototype and have a real conversation than a polished deck that doesn't reflect reality.'

#### With Industry Peers / Thought Leadership

- 'Technology is supposed to make people more capable. Too often it makes them feel stupid. That's backwards, and I'm not willing to accept it.'

- 'This shift is different from the ones before it. The machine has intelligence now. What changed isn't the interface, it's the relationship between the person and the system.'

- 'Strong to superb is the standard I hold myself to. Not perfect. Superb is about what it enables, not how it looks.'

#### On How You Lead

- 'My job is to remove the obstacles between talented people and great work.'

- 'I will be direct with you because I respect you. If I'm not being direct, that's when you should worry.'

- 'Accountability isn't a consequence. It's a covenant. We both have to honor it.'

### What to Amplify

Based on your 360 feedback and brand positioning, these are the themes to lean into more publicly:

- The research instinct. You see the person behind the data. That belongs in external conversations, not just internal ones.

- The turnaround narrative. You've stabilized distressed teams by building trust first. Tell it that way. The trust is the mechanism, not a nice detail.

- The human thesis. Technology should make people more capable. That belief is the through-line of your whole career and the most defensible thing you own.

- Translating human outcomes into business terms. Executives need that bridge, and you are one of the few people who can build it in both directions.

## 05 · Brand in Action

*How you operate.*

### Executive Presence

Presence is not volume. It's the quality of attention you give and the quality of attention you draw. Your 360 puts you at the top of the room on integrity, compassion, and courage. Those are the anchors. Everything else is technique.

- Enter prepared. Know the business context before any conversation with a senior leader.

- Point first. State your recommendation, then your rationale. Not the other way around.

- Pause before responding. The two seconds of silence signals you're thinking, not reacting.

- Use your research training. You see patterns others miss. Name them. That's the superpower.

- Listen first. You are a rare listener, and in an advisory room that is most of the job. What you hear is what makes the recommendation land.

### Written Communications

- Regular cadence with the people you lead and the audience you're building. Consistency was a noted differentiator inside your teams and it works the same way outside a company.

- Exec updates: bottom line up front. Three bullets max for status; one clear ask.

- LinkedIn and thought leadership: your point of view, stated plainly. Provoke thinking. Short is more shareable than long.

- Proposals and strategy docs: structure is the argument. Clear headings, clear recommendations, evidence-backed.

### Behavioral Anchors

Your brand lives in behavior more than words. These are the non-negotiables:

| Anchor | Behavior |
|---|---|
| **Do the right thing** | Even when it's unpopular. Even when it costs you. Your 360 names this as a superpower. Protect it. |
| **Close the loop** | You evolve your thinking through multiple conversations without always looping back. Build the habit of explicit closing. |
| **Trust the people with you** | You default to trust with everyone else. Extend it to your collaborators and release the details. Your scope depends on it. |
| **Name what you see** | You read people and systems faster than almost anyone. Saying the pattern out loud is a gift to the person who can't see it yet. Don't sit on it. |
| **Protect your calendar** | Others are managing your time. Reclaim it. Deep work and strategic relationships require protected space. |
| **Amplify impact** | Not self-promotion. Narrating value clearly is a leadership responsibility. 'Here's what we built. Here's what it changed.' |

## 06 · Future Brand

*Building what's next.*

You are weighing two paths: leading people inside an organization, and a leadership advisory built on what you do best. The foundation in this document works for both, which was always the point. This section frames the forward-facing layer.

### The Hedgehog

Three circles, held separately at their proper altitude. This version is anchored in leadership. The earlier one was built when the plan was an advisory practice, and it aimed at converting skeptics rather than developing people.

#### Passion

Leadership is supposed to make people more capable. Too often it makes them feel small. That is backwards, and you refuse to accept it. Same conviction you hold about technology, pointed at the thing you actually do.

#### Best in the world

Telling people the truth in a way that leaves them more capable than they were before you said it. Most leaders are kind and vague or clear and diminishing. You are clear and elevating. Thirty years of receipts sit under that, and it is rare enough to build a practice on.

#### Economic engine

Return per leader you develop who then develops others. Not per engagement, not per title, not per revenue dollar. The multiplier is the denominator, which is what makes it a discipline: it excludes any work where you are the hero and nothing compounds after you leave.

#### The guardrail

The risk is taking rooms that want your clarity but not your people work. You would be excellent at it, the money would be good, and it would quietly hollow out the thing that makes you you.

### Future Brand Positioning

> I help leaders, and the organizations around them, close the gap between strong and superb. Evidence-based strategy, judgment and capability development, and the kind of candid partnership most people won't offer.
