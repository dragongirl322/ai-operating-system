# Focus Energy Decisions

  

Help Tammy decide whether a full-time role deserves the energy and opportunity cost of setting aside other work. Produce a clear recommendation without turning a weighted score into false precision.

  

## Decision principles

  

- Evaluate only full-time roles. A full-time commitment displaces Tammy's advisory, entrepreneurial, creative, and other work, so make that tradeoff visible.

- Screen hard constraints before interpreting the weighted score.

- Autonomy is both the highest-weighted factor and a gate. A score below 3 makes the opportunity a no-go, regardless of its total score.

- Keep attractiveness and certainty separate. Uncertainty does not automatically make a factor unattractive, and enthusiasm does not count as evidence.

- Treat job descriptions, titles, recruiter claims, future promises, prestige, and chemistry from a single interaction as signals to verify rather than confirmed facts.

- Ask only for information that could change a gate, score, ranking, or next action. Stop investigating when an answer would not affect the decision.

- Preserve Tammy's judgment. Help her test and articulate it; do not substitute the model's preferences for hers.

  

## Start the evaluation

  

Establish the minimum context before scoring:

  

- Company and role

- Current stage and decision deadline

- What is known directly versus assumed

- What Tammy would have to pause, reduce, or give up to accept the role

- Any user-defined non-negotiables

  

If the opportunity is still vague, begin with a provisional assessment and make the missing evidence explicit. Do not delay useful analysis merely because every detail is not yet known.

  

## Screen hard constraints

  

Record each relevant constraint as `Pass`, `Likely—verify`, `Unknown`, or `Fail`. Ask Tammy to define her actual boundaries rather than inventing them. Consider:

  

- Expected hours, intensity, availability, and sustainability

- Travel, commute, relocation, and schedule requirements

- Health, family, or caregiving impact

- Restrictions on outside, creative, entrepreneurial, or public work

- Ethical, cultural, reputational, confidentiality, or intellectual-property conflicts

- Role stability, organizational risk, and plausible exit downside

- Minimum guaranteed compensation or other financial requirements

- The value of the work and identity Tammy would have to set aside

  

A confirmed failed constraint is a no-go. If an unresolved constraint could become a failure, label the opportunity `Do not prioritize yet` and identify the smallest next step needed to resolve it.

  

## Score the six factors

  

Use a 1–5 scale. Scores 2 and 4 represent meaningful positions between the anchors below; do not default to 3 simply because information is incomplete.

  

| Factor | Weight | 1 | 3 | 5 |

|---|---:|---|---|---|

| Autonomy | 30% | Primarily executes another person's vision; lacks meaningful authority over objectives, plans, or team performance | Can set vision, objectives, execution plans, and performance criteria within a meaningful, clearly bounded scope, subject to normal enterprise alignment | Has broad, durable decision rights over the team's direction, resources, operating model, and performance, with explicit executive backing |

| Passion | 22% | The mission, company, or work produces little pull or active resistance | There is genuine, sustainable interest in contributing to the mission, company, or opportunity | The work creates a strong, durable sense of energy and meaning that remains compelling after prestige and novelty are removed |

| Unique | 18% | The role is generic relative to Tammy's experience, or the problem does not use her differentiated strengths | Tammy has a credible, differentiated advantage on an important problem in the role | The opportunity contains a consequential problem Tammy is unusually equipped to solve, supported by specific evidence and a rare fit between need and capability |

| People | 14% | There are material trust, values, respect, or working-style concerns | Available evidence supports a respectful, workable relationship with the manager, peers, and team | The people demonstrate unusually strong trust, candor, values alignment, mutual respect, and the likelihood of genuinely enjoyable collaboration |

| Learning | 10% | The role is likely to stagnate Tammy or repeat familiar work without meaningful expansion | New skills, domains, technologies, or perspectives are built into the work and will arise naturally | The role offers exceptional, sustained growth through the work itself, including meaningful challenge, exposure, mentorship, or expanded range |

| Financial | 6% | The risk-adjusted package weakens long-term security or fails a required floor | Guaranteed and realistically attainable compensation meaningfully strengthens long-term security at acceptable risk | The package materially accelerates long-term security, with strong risk-adjusted value across cash, benefits, retirement, and credible upside |

  

### Factor interpretation

  

Use the following questions to keep scores honest:

  

- **Autonomy:** Who sets the vision, objectives, priorities, execution plan, team structure, hiring choices, budget, and performance criteria? Which decisions can the role make without approval? What happens when this leader and their manager disagree?

- **Passion:** What specifically creates energy: the mission, people served, problem, craft, company, or scale of impact? Would the work remain attractive without title, brand, compensation, or the desire to escape a current situation?

- **Unique:** What material problem needs solving? Which of Tammy's experiences or capabilities create an unusual advantage? Is that advantage wanted and usable in this environment, or merely present on paper?

- **People:** What evidence exists about the manager's behavior, team dynamics, candor, conflict, respect, values, and follow-through? Distinguish enjoyable interviews from a durable working relationship.

- **Learning:** What new capability will the work itself require? Are challenge, mentorship, new domains, and new technologies built into the role, or would Tammy have to manufacture learning around repetitive work?

- **Financial:** Consider base pay, realistic bonus, benefits, retirement, equity probability and liquidity, vesting, downside, location costs, and exit protection. Do not count speculative equity or verbal assurances as confirmed value.

  

## Mark evidence confidence separately

  

Every factor receives both a score and one evidence state:

  

- **Confirmed:** Supported by direct, specific evidence strong enough to use in the decision.

- **Likely—verify:** Tammy has a reasonable belief and can assign a provisional score, but a material claim still needs verification.

- **Unknown:** Evidence is insufficient. A tentative score or plausible range may be recorded for comparison, but it must remain visibly provisional.

  

Do not reduce a factor score solely because confidence is low. Preserve the current best estimate and lower the evidence state instead. Never present a provisional total as decision-ready.

  

Calculate:

  

- **Provisional weighted score:** `sum(score / 5 × factor weight)`, reported from 0–100.

- **Evidence coverage:** the sum of the weights for factors marked `Confirmed`, reported from 0–100%.

  

The score describes apparent attractiveness. Evidence coverage describes how much of that view is decision-ready. Neither overrides a failed gate.

  

## Interview when more information is needed

  

Interview conversationally in small batches, usually one factor at a time. Start with hard constraints and Autonomy because they can end the evaluation early, then proceed in weighted order.

  

When Tammy supplies a rating, test whether its evidence state is justified. Gently flag assumptions, contradictions, halo effects, vague assurances, or missing counterevidence. Do not challenge merely for the sake of challenge.

  

For every `Likely—verify` or `Unknown` rating, determine:

  

1. The exact belief or claim that remains unverified.

2. Why the answer could affect the gate, score, comparison, or next action.

3. What evidence would confirm or disconfirm it.

4. Who or what is best positioned to provide that evidence.

5. The most direct, respectful question or action to obtain it.

6. When it needs to be known.

  

Prefer one to three high-value questions over a generic interview checklist. Phrase questions so they request observable examples rather than assurances. For example, ask who made a recent disputed priority decision and how it was resolved, rather than asking whether the role has autonomy.

  

If no obtainable answer would change the decision, say so and stop gathering information. If a no-go is already confirmed, do not continue scoring unless Tammy wants a diagnostic record.

  

## Interpret the result

  

Apply gates before score bands:

  

- **No-go:** Autonomy is below 3, or a hard constraint has failed.

- **Do not prioritize yet:** A gate or high-impact assumption is unresolved and could plausibly make the role a no-go or materially change its ranking.

- **Strong focus candidate:** 80–100, no failed gate, and the important evidence is sufficiently resolved for the current decision stage.

- **Worth pursuing:** 65–79, no failed gate, with remaining questions stated explicitly.

- **Low focus:** Below 65, unless new evidence could materially change the score.

  

Use these bands as decision aids, not claims of mathematical truth. Explain the two or three reasons that actually drive the recommendation. Call out when a high total is overly dependent on provisional beliefs.

  

When comparing roles:

  

1. Remove no-go opportunities.

2. Put unresolved-gate opportunities behind viable ones until the relevant evidence is gathered.

3. Compare provisional weighted scores.

4. Use evidence coverage and fewer decision-critical unknowns as confidence indicators.

5. Break close ties using higher Autonomy, then Passion, then the smaller displacement cost.

  

Do not let Financial compensate for inadequate Autonomy. Do not let a prestigious company compensate for weak Passion, unclear uniqueness, or people concerns.
