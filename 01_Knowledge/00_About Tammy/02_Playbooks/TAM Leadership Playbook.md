## Leadership Principles

TAM is the system. These are the terms it operates under. Before strategy, before mastery, before any outcome, these four govern how I use all of it.

### Human Outcomes First

The measure of the work has never been the artifact. It's whether the person on the other side of it ends up more capable, more confident, and more free. When a decision is close, this is the tiebreaker: not what's fastest, not what's technically superior — what leaves people better off for having gone through it.

### Value, Worth, and Voice

I take what's already strong in a person, a team, or a system and help it become superb. People should leave my orbit knowing what they're good at and willing to say so out loud. Value flows in sequence, not hierarchy: for each other first, because the team and the partners around me are the condition that makes everything else possible; for the company second, because organizational value is what those human conditions produce; for customers always, because that's the purpose the entire system serves.

### Trust First, Truth Always

I default to trust. When someone falls short, I assume a gap before I assume a failing — first in understanding, then in skill, never in character. I find the gap and close it. The hard thing still gets said. It lands as care instead of criticism, and that's why people trust me back.

### Clarity as an Act of Respect

I cut through complexity and say what matters plainly. Goals stated. Expectations clear. Ownership named. Ambiguity is a cost I refuse to pass on to the people around me.

## The Responsibility Rings

Leadership Principles govern how I act. This is how I decide who gets my attention first when I can't give everyone the whole of it.

![The Responsibility Rings](https://claude.ai/chat/responsibility_rings.png)

Priority here isn't a ranking of who matters most. Every ring matters. It's a sequence: when something has to give, this is the order it gives in, and the order that gets protected no matter what.

I think of it in rings, not a ladder, because none of this resolves once and stays resolved. Every ring needs tending today, and again tomorrow. What the order determines isn't who I care about. It's who gets the whole version of me and who gets what's left over.

**Ring 1 — Self.** Everything else draws from this one. A self that isn't whole produces a diminished version of itself at every ring outward: a worse parent, a worse partner, a worse leader. Protecting this ring isn't indulgence. It's the only way the rest of them get the real thing instead of the leftovers.

**Ring 2 — Responsible For.** The people whose outcomes depend on my decisions and who didn't fully choose the arrangement: kids, the team that reports to me. They hold less power in the relationship than I do. That's exactly why they come this early, and why this ring doesn't wait.

**Ring 3 — Responsible With.** The people who share the load and the outcome: a spouse, cross-functional partners. This ring runs on reciprocity, not obligation, which means it can absorb more patience and more repair than Ring 2 can.

**Ring 4 — Broader Community.** Everyone else with a legitimate claim on my time: the family I grew up in, my town, my bosses, the company. Real, and worth showing up for. But Ring 4 is usually the loudest ring, and it's tempting to answer the loud thing first. That's usually the mistake.

> _**This is where I start when I'm coaching someone through overwhelm or burnout. It's almost never a Ring 4 problem. Almost always, Ring 1 or Ring 2 has been quietly deprioritized in favor of whatever in Ring 4 was making the most noise. Fix the order before you fix the calendar.**_

**TRENCHANT:** Strategic clarity that cuts through complexity and defines direction

**ANCHORED:** Inquiry and evidence that ground every decision in what is true

## Mastery: The Foundation Beneath the Lenses

Success is a point you arrive at. Mastery is a practice you never finish — always reaching for what's just past your grasp, using what you almost got to go further next time. That distinction, from Sarah Lewis's _The Rise_, is the foundation everything else here stands on. Not craft as polish. Not craft as taste. Mastery as the discipline of always reaching.

Without it, strategy produces nothing worth experiencing. But mastery is an enabler, not the destination. The value of the work never lived in the artifact's quality. It lives in what the artifact makes possible for the people who experience it and the organizations that deliver it.

Mastery in service of the North Star. That is the only pursuit of it worth making.

#### Principles That Govern Mastery

- **Honor People:** At the heart of every decision is a person. To pursue mastery is to understand their goals, behaviors, and needs deeply. It begins and ends with people in mind.
- **Reach for the Near-Perfect:** Mastery is a journey, not a destination. It is the constant pursuit of growth: pushing boundaries, refining skill, and treating the “near-perfect” as fuel for the next attempt rather than a finish line.
- **Cherish Iteration:** The path to excellence is non-linear. It demands resilience, curiosity, and a willingness to refine until we achieve clarity, balance, and utility.
- **Balance Beauty and Functionality:** Mastery thrives where form meets function. Real excellence creates experiences that are as delightful as they are effective, where beauty enhances purpose instead of competing with it.
- **Catalyze Collaboration:** The real strength is in collaboration built around the human experience — bringing together diverse perspectives and disciplines, united by a shared focus on understanding and serving people.
- **Create with Intent:** Every choice, big or small, has to align with purpose. Mastery is intentional. Each detail earns its place in the whole.

## The Operating Model

### TRENCHANT — Strategic Clarity

_Strategic clarity that cuts through complexity and defines direction._

#### 1. Vision and Objective Setting

Define a compelling vision aligned to business goals. Translate vision into strategic objectives and measurable results. Support teams in determining how to achieve them; provide direction on the how when necessary — for both the UX org and partners in product and engineering.

#### 2. Enterprise Alignment

Understand and align to the vision and objectives of the business. Map how each major business pillar connects to that vision. Ensure design strategy serves and strengthens every pillar, not just the product organization.

#### 3. Experience as Business Strategy

Experience exists everywhere, not just in product. Customers experience the organization through advertising, sales practices, physical interactions, digital interactions, and everything in between. Whether those experiences are deliberate or accidental decides how much value customers receive.

#### 4. The Full Experience Ecosystem

Account for what must be true across the entire customer journey:

- Brand side — awareness, belief
- Product side — functional quality, usability, appeal, value
- Go-to-market side — how the brand shows up before and after the product interaction

#### 5. Prioritization Frameworks

Help the business make better prioritization decisions through evidence and frameworks. Quantify and describe the outcomes customers value most. Assess organizational performance compared to competitors on those outcomes. Show where the organization has the highest likelihood of winning. Connect design investment directly to strategic opportunity.

#### 6. Platform Thinking and Network Effects

Apply platform principles and network effects to experience strategy. Map every constituent in the ecosystem — internal teams, external customers, partners — and what each values. Design for how value compounds as the system grows, not just how individual interactions perform.

### ANCHORED — Inquiry and Evidence

_Inquiry and evidence that ground every decision in what is true._

#### 1. Constituent Intelligence

Map and deeply understand every constituent in the ecosystem: customers, internal teams, partners. Define what each constituent needs, values, and experiences. This is not a one-time exercise; it is an evergreen system.

#### 2. Outcome-Based Prioritization

Use evidence to identify the outcomes customers value most. Assess current performance against those outcomes relative to competitors. Name where the organization has the highest probability of winning. Connect those opportunities directly to business prioritization decisions.

#### 3. Brand Effectiveness Framework

A strong brand is an attractor that drives growth. Measure and manage:

- Known — is the brand recognized?
- Distinct — does it stand apart from competitors?
- Understood — is the messaging clear?
- Believed — is the messaging credible?
- Preferred — do people choose it over alternatives?

_These are not soft metrics. They are leading indicators of business growth._

#### 4. Product Experience Hierarchy

Not all experience quality is equal. There is a clear hierarchy:

- Functional, reliable, secure — table stakes; without these nothing else matters
- Usable and accessible — necessary for any customer to derive value
- Appealing — drives preference and positive emotion
- Loved and preferred — the compounding result of brand and product excellence coming together

_Diagnostic decisions must respect this hierarchy. Fix lower levels before improving higher ones._

#### 5. Intelligence Systems

Build and maintain systems that house what the organization knows about customers, partners, and the competitive landscape. In the age of AI, these systems must be queryable for immediate, actionable intelligence. Ground them in grounded theory method. Make them accessible across the enterprise, not siloed within the design organization.

#### 6. Measurement and Effectiveness Tracking

Maintain an evergreen system for measuring overall brand and product quality. Track the effectiveness of ways of working — is the organization becoming more experience and design obsessed? Where are the gaps? How do they get closed? What is measured gets managed.

**Communication as a Leadership Discipline**

Communicate consistently and deliberately about what teams should be thinking about, doing, and changing to improve business results through experience investment. Silence creates confusion. Clarity creates momentum.

#### Vector 2: Enterprise Conditions

_The Steel Thread in practice._

**Relationship Architecture**

Understand the goals, motivations, and ways of working of every key partner across the enterprise. Meet them where they are. Work in partnership rather than in competition. Their interest is my interest. Their success is my success.

**The Steel Thread in Practice**

Experience threads through every function. Position design not as a service function requesting access but as the connective force that makes every function's work more intentional and more valuable.

**Structural Influence**

This requires patience, strategic positioning, coalition building, and the willingness to work through systems rather than around them. Results take longer to become visible, but the conditions they create compound over time.

**Design Obsession Across the Enterprise**

A core responsibility is helping the entire organization become experience and design obsessed, not just the design team. This means thought leadership, education, frameworks, and communication that make great experience everyone's priority. Measure it. Close the gaps where it is missing.

**The Coherent Architecture**

Deliver the coherent architecture that enables the Single Story. Ensure all touch points — end to end — fit together in a way that feels seamless to customers. Cohesion strengthens brand awareness, reduces cognitive load, increases positive emotion, and builds confidence in the quality of the brand and its products.

**Design Systems at Scale**

A robust design system is critical infrastructure at the enterprise level. Subsystems may exist for specific user types or business pillars, but every subsystem must keep coherence with the parent system. The design system is the physical embodiment of the Coherent Architecture.

## The Accomplishment Framework

Every career story follows four moves in sequence:

- **DIAGNOSE** — What was broken, missing, or underperforming? What did the evidence show?
- **ARCHITECT** — What framework, system, or strategy did you define to address it?
- **ALIGN** — Who did you need to bring with you, and how did you get them there?
- **DELIVER** — What changed as a measurable result?

**Mapped to TAM:**

- TRENCHANT stories — lead with the business problem, not the design solution. The design work is the method. The business outcome is the headline.
- ANCHORED stories — lead with what you did not know, what you built to find out, and what became possible because you now knew it.
- MEANINGFUL stories — lead with the human condition that was broken, what you designed to fix it, and how people responded.
- STEEL THREAD stories — lead with the outcome that surprised people. The business result they did not expect a design leader to deliver.
- COHERENT ARCHITECTURE stories — lead with what was not possible before you built it, and what became possible after.

#### LOGOS — Give Them the Logic

Here is what I need you to reconsider before you leave today.

Saying that UX and design are only about the way a product looks and feels is like saying a woman's place is in the kitchen. It is wrong. It is limiting. And it is costing you.

Your customers are interacting with your brand and your products right now — whether you are being intentional about it or not. Every interaction is either designed or accidental. Every touchpoint either tells a coherent story or a fragmented one. Every experience either moves someone closer to what they need or further away from it.

The organizations that understand this — that treat experience as a strategic business capability rather than a finishing coat of paint — are the ones that earn repeat customers, command premium pricing, and build brands people actually love.

The measure of great design is not the artifact. It is what the artifact makes possible.

> _**Every person who experiences what we build should find themselves more capable, more confident, and more free.**_

That is not an aspiration. That is a standard. And it is the only standard worth building to.”
