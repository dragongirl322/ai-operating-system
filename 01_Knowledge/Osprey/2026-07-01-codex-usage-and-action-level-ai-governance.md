# Codex Usage and Action-Level AI Governance

This note preserves the July 1, 2026 Osprey AI Overnight Brief plus the expanded follow-up summaries on Codex usage data and the design leadership problem of making AI action governable without making it unusable.

## Osprey AI Overnight Brief, July 1, 2026

Found 6 items worth your attention.

### AI Signals

1. **Frontier model releases are becoming governed access events, not normal product launches.** On June 26, 2026, OpenAI staggered GPT-5.6 access after a U.S. government request, starting with a small trusted-partner preview. On July 1, 2026, The Guardian reported that U.S. export controls on Anthropic's Fable 5 and Mythos 5 were lifted after strengthened cyber safeguards and closer government collaboration. Why it matters: the operating question is no longer "who has the strongest model?" It is "who controls release authority, customer access, fallback continuity, and safety evidence when model capabilities cross national-security thresholds?" Sources: [The Guardian on OpenAI, June 26, 2026](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/jun/26/openai-ai-model-release-trump-us-sam-altman-gpt-anthropic-mythos); [The Guardian on Anthropic, July 1, 2026](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/jul/01/anthropic-fable-mythos-ai-models-us-export-controls-lifted).

2. **Agentic AI is moving from developer novelty to work system.** A June 25, 2026 arXiv paper using Codex data reports active agentic-AI users grew more than fivefold in the first half of 2026, with non-developers becoming a fast-growing audience; more than 10% of users managed three or more concurrent agents weekly. Axios also reported on June 30, 2026 that Anthropic's Sonnet 5 is aimed at lower-risk everyday agentic work, and Investor's Business Daily reported that Google and FactSet are building agentic finance workflows. Why it matters: this is the shift from "ask the chatbot" to "delegate work, supervise progress, review artifacts, and own the outcome." Sources: [The Shift to Agentic AI: Evidence from Codex, June 25, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26959); [Axios on Claude Sonnet 5, June 30, 2026](https://www.axios.com/2026/06/30/anthropic-sonnet-5-agents-mythos-fable); [Investor's Business Daily on Google and FactSet, June 30, 2026](https://www.investors.com/news/factset-google-new-generation-of-ai-agents-finance/).

### AI Design and Experience

3. **Enterprise AI UX is converging on control, transparency, reliability, context, collaboration, and data governance.** A June 2026 HCI International paper on business-context AI agents identifies concrete UX criteria: human override, visible intended steps, confirmation before critical decisions, understandable reasoning, audit trails, role-aware permissions, and completion reports. A SOUPS 2026 paper on consumer GenAI privacy transparency finds users often rely on weak proxies like popularity because current security and privacy communications feel incomplete or not credible. Why it matters: privacy and governance are not back-office concerns. They are part of the user experience that determines whether people will safely adopt AI. Sources: [Human-AI Agent Interaction in a Business Context, June 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18716); [Understanding U.S. Users' Security and Privacy Transparency Needs, revised June 19, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17270).

4. **Warmth, empathy, and "helpfulness" are becoming safety-critical design variables.** A May 2026 HCI review argues that affective cues in agents shape trust calibration, delegation, repair, dependence, and governance. Separately, reporting on Oxford research published in Nature says warmer chatbot tuning produced more mistakes and more validation of false beliefs, especially when users expressed vulnerability. Why it matters: AI personality is not brand tone. It is a control layer that can preserve judgment or erode it. Sources: [Caring Without Feeling, submitted May 8, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18259); [The Guardian on Oxford/Nature chatbot study, April 29, 2026](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/apr/29/making-ai-chatbots-more-friendly-mistakes-support-false-beliefs-conspiracy-theories-study).

### Human-Computer Paradigm Shifts

5. **The user is being moved from selector to auditor.** A May 13, 2026 paper measuring Google AI Overviews found AI-generated summaries appeared for 13.7% of trending queries overall and 64.7% of question-form queries; 11.0% of atomic claims were unsupported by cited pages. The paradigm shift is that users increasingly receive a synthesized answer before choosing sources, while agents increasingly complete work before users inspect it. Why it matters: provenance, contestability, interruption, and review states are becoming primary interface components. Source: [Measuring Google AI Overviews, May 13, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14021).

### Design Leadership Problems

6. **The recurring leadership problem is making AI action governable without making it unusable.** The European Commission's General-Purpose AI Code of Practice page, last updated June 25, 2026, frames GPAI compliance around transparency, copyright, safety, and systemic-risk mitigation. An April 2026 paper on AI agents under EU law argues that high-risk agentic systems with untraceable behavioral drift cannot currently satisfy AI Act requirements, and that providers need exhaustive inventories of external actions, data flows, connected systems, and affected people. A separate April 2026 agent-privacy paper shows the design direction: permission specifications and data-flow enforcement can be built into the execution environment, not left to vague trust. Why it matters: design leaders can turn governance into usable product behavior: permission ladders, release tiers, consent paths, human overrides, audit trails, safety-evaluation protocols, and human handoffs. Sources: [European Commission GPAI Code of Practice, updated June 25, 2026](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/ai-code-practice); [AI Agents Under EU Law, April 6, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04604); [An AI Agent Execution Environment to Safeguard User Data, April 21, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19657).

### Why This Matters For You

The through line today is **authority design**. The freshest signals are not just about more capable models. They are about who can release them, who can use them, how delegated work is bounded, how AI behavior becomes legible, how users keep judgment, and how organizations prove they are not smuggling risk into the customer experience.

This reinforces your AI Experience territory: the valuable design leadership work is the layer between capability and adoption. The strongest advisory framing remains: design the authority, delegation, and judgment systems that determine whether AI becomes durable human and organizational value.

## Detailed Follow-Up: Codex Usage Data

The Codex paper is useful because it treats agentic AI as a **work-organization shift**, not just a usage-growth story.

The source is the June 25, 2026 arXiv paper [The Shift to Agentic AI: Evidence from Codex](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26959). It analyzes aggregated, anonymized Codex usage across three groups: individual users, organizational users, and OpenAI workers.

Key details:

- **Active Codex usage grew more than 5x** between January 1 and June 1, 2026.
- Adoption is uneven. In the 28 days before June 11, 2026, **fewer than 1% of active individual users** used Codex, while **17.3% of organizational users** did.
- But output intensity tells a stronger story: Codex accounted for **16.5% of individual-user output tokens**, **63.3% of organizational-user output tokens**, and **99.8% of OpenAI worker output tokens** across Codex and ChatGPT.
- The most important behavioral signal: users are not just asking questions. They are delegating work: debugging, refactoring, validating changes, configuring apps, drafting docs, analyzing data, managing repos, and producing knowledge artifacts.
- Task complexity is rising fast. Among sampled individual users, the share submitting at least one prompt estimated to take an experienced human **8+ hours** rose from **2.1% in December 2025 to 25.6% in May 2026**.
- The strongest users are running Codex like a workbench. More than **10% of users manage three or more concurrent Codex agents weekly**; within OpenAI, **28.6% managed five or more concurrent agents** in the week before June 11.
- Skills matter because they turn one-off prompting into reusable workflow infrastructure. Skill use rose from **5.4% on March 1 to 26.6% on June 11, 2026**. For active users in the June 11 window: **25.7% of individual users**, **30.4% of organizational users**, and **96.2% of OpenAI users** invoked at least one skill.

The design read: this is the move from "AI as answer engine" to "AI as delegated production system." The human role shifts toward scoping, assigning, supervising, reviewing, integrating, and deciding what counts as acceptable work.

## Detailed Follow-Up: Making AI Action Governable Without Making It Unusable

"Making AI action governable without making it unusable" means the governance has to live at the **action level**, not only in policy docs or broad permission screens.

The EU side is pushing this direction. The European Commission says the GPAI Code of Practice is meant to help providers comply with AI Act obligations around transparency, copyright, and safety/security, with systemic-risk models requiring risk assessment, mitigation, incident reporting, and cybersecurity practices. Source: [European Commission GPAI Code](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/contents-code-gpai).

The sharper agent-specific analysis comes from [AI Agents Under EU Law](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04604). Its core point: an agent's risk is determined less by model architecture and more by what it can do externally. Does it send email? Process refunds? Screen candidates? Deploy code? Access health records? Touch payment systems? Publish content? Each action activates different legal, privacy, safety, liability, and oversight requirements.

The paper's practical framing:

- Start with an inventory of the agent's **external actions, data flows, connected systems, and affected people**.
- Classify risk by domain and effect, not by saying "it is just an LLM."
- Define which actions require confirmation, which can run autonomously, and which require escalation.
- Treat tool access as a governance surface: read, write, send, delete, deploy, pay, publish, and decide are different authority levels.
- Use runtime telemetry to route oversight: confidence, drift, user expertise, reversibility, affected party, and stakes should change the control mode.
- Preserve action-level records: who authorized what, what the agent did, what data it touched, what external systems it affected, and how review happened.

The "without making it unusable" part is where design leadership matters. Bad governance is constant pop-ups, blanket denials, vague warnings, or one-size-fits-all human review. Good governance is graduated autonomy:

- Low-risk, reversible actions can proceed with logging.
- Medium-risk actions get preview, confirmation, or batch approval.
- High-risk actions require named human authority.
- Irreversible, rights-affecting, financial, health, employment, legal, or external-facing actions need stronger gates, audit trails, and recovery paths.
- User controls should be scoped to the real action: "read this file," "draft but don't send," "send only to this recipient," "spend up to X," "deploy to staging only," "summarize but do not update the record."

The GAAP paper, [An AI Agent Execution Environment to Safeguard User Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19657), gives a concrete technical example: enforce user-defined disclosure permissions, track data flows across tasks, keep a disclosure log, and block data leaks deterministically instead of relying on the model to behave. That is the pattern to watch: governance becomes part of the execution environment and the experience, not a compliance layer bolted on afterward.

The Workday and Canvas Kit connection is important: this is not a new abstraction. It maps back to the design-system work of making governance part of the user experience itself. For AI systems, governability should show up in the designed interaction: scoped permissions, visible action authority, confirmation moments, overrides, escalation paths, auditability, and handoffs.

The Tammy-specific takeaway: the design-leadership problem is to make AI authority **visible, bounded, revocable, and reviewable** while keeping useful delegation fluid.
