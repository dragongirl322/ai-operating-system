“AI is absolutely a civilizational technology.”  (In interview with Tim Ferris

She then expands the definition:

“It’ll have or already [be] having a profound impact in the economic, social, cultural, political… effects of our society.”  

She was arguing that most conversations about AI are too narrowly focused on:

- technical capability,
    
- startup growth,
    
- market hype,
    
- or existential fear.
    

Her point was that AI is becoming infrastructure-level technology — more comparable to:

- electricity,
    
- the internet,
    
- literacy,
    
- or computing itself.
    

Not just “another software wave.”

She pivots away from technology and toward human agency:

“People are at the heart of everything.”  

Then she warns that:

- people must retain dignity,
    
- people must retain agency,
    
- and people must feel they have a place in the future AI creates.  
    

That’s the core of her worldview: AI is transformational enough to reshape civilization, but dangerous if humanity becomes psychologically or structurally displaced inside those systems.
