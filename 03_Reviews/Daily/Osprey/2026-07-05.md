## AI Signals

1. **Frontier-AI governance is now a product-access and ownership question, not only a safety-policy question.** The Guardian reported on July 2, 2026 that U.S. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick had discussed whether taxpayers should get a stake in OpenAI after federal AI infrastructure support; Times of India reported on July 5 that no similar government stake is currently on the table for Anthropic after U.S. officials cleared Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5 access. Sources: [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/jul/02/us-government-openai-stake-howard-lutnick), [Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/technology/tech-news/after-trump-administration-clears-anthropics-claude-fable-5-and-mythos-5-no-sam-altmans-openai-like-government-stake-on-table/articleshow/132181755.cms).

Treat this as a competitive signal, not proof. The useful point for Tammy: as labs move faster, design leaders should ask what evidence travels with capability claims: independent evaluation, deployment constraints, user-facing failure modes, and whether the experience makes limits legible. ## AI Design and Experience

3. **Agent permissions are becoming an experience-design problem, not a settings-page problem.** A July 1, 2026 arXiv paper, *Janus: a Playground for User-Involved Agentic Permission Management*, tested different permission-assistant designs and found that user input can materially improve privacy and security, but permission fatigue and context differences make one-size-fits-all controls brittle. Design implication: agent permissions need proportional consent, scoped delegation, decision aids, revocation, and recovery paths that match the risk of the action. Source: [arXiv:2607.01510](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01510).

4. **AI "personality" and AI persona research need stronger validity checks.** Two July 2, 2026 arXiv papers point in the same direction. *Personality Without Persons?* finds that Big Five-style personality scores do not measure LLMs the way they measure humans and can create misleading governance claims. A visualization-design study finds LLM personas can be useful exploratory probes, but outputs are highly model- and task-dependent and should not substitute for human participants. Design implication: anthropomorphic labels can make AI behavior feel legible while weakening actual evidence. Sources: [arXiv:2607.02325](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02325), [arXiv:2607.02455](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02455).

## Human-Computer Paradigm Shifts

5. **"Human-AI team" is too vague to guide design.** A July 2, 2026 arXiv review of 53 human-AI teaming papers found multiple team types being grouped under the same label, including assistant-like relationships, forced dependencies, and more equal team structures. The authors argue that findings may not transfer cleanly across team types. Paradigm implication: organizations need to name the relationship they are designing: tool, assistant, delegate, supervisor, peer, broker, or accountable actor. Source: [arXiv:2607.02198](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02198).

6. **AI-mediated social interaction is becoming persuasive infrastructure.** A July 2, 2026 arXiv paper reports five preregistered studies in which short conversations with AI chatbots representing a political outgroup reduced cross-partisan animosity and increased willingness to have a real conversation, though much of the warmth effect faded within a week. This is promising and risky at the same time: AI can lower barriers to human contact, but it can also become a scalable influence layer. Source: [arXiv:2607.02181](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02181).

## Design Leadership Problems

- **Designing permission without exhaustion:** users need real agency over agent action, but constant permission prompts can become theater.
- **Separating valid evidence from human-like labels:** "personality," "persona," "trustworthy," and "collaborative" need proof structures, not vibes.
- **Classifying the human-AI relationship before designing it:** a delegated actor, a tutor, a reviewer, and a teammate need different authority, feedback, and accountability models.
- **Making influence auditable:** AI systems that change beliefs, reduce conflict, or mediate sensitive conversations need disclosure, monitoring, opt-out, and escalation paths.
- **Carrying evidence with frontier capability:** model claims should come with deployment limits, failure modes, evaluation provenance, and human-facing control surfaces.

## Why This Matters For You

Today's through-line is sharper than "AI is getting better." The live problem is **evidence-backed delegation**: when AI acts, advises, persuades, represents a user, or stands in for another person, the experience has to show what authority it has, what evidence supports it, what role it is playing, and how a human can contest or redirect it.

That fits your AI Experience and organization-transformation territory directly. Your trust-layer framing is useful because it refuses to separate customer UX from builder governance. The emerging leadership need is to turn slippery ideas like autonomy, personality, collaboration, safety, and trust into designed operating conditions people can actually inspect, use, and govern.
