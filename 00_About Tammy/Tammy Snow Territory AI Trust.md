The problem

Enterprises are pouring capital into AI and getting little back. Most leaders already know the bottleneck isn’t the technology. What they’re missing is more specific: AI changes the lived experience of work, and that experience runs in two directions at once.

Inside the organization, the teams building AI products can be fully fluent in the tools and still ship something no customer trusts. Building AI people rely on takes different skills than these teams built their careers on: less executing a known design, more judgment about what’s worth building, when to trust the output, and how to design for a system that behaves differently every time. That judgment is the gap, and it doesn’t stay inside. It ships straight to the customer.

Outside, the customer meets what those teams ship and decides whether to trust it at all. That second experience is only as good as the first. No one owns either side, and no one connects them.

Why the current approach misses it

The firms selling AI transformation run these as separate, scaled workstreams: workforce training in one place, customer experience in another. Each delivered as a program, neither owned as an experience anyone trusts. The link between them goes unowned entirely. So the 1% maturity rate isn’t a technology gap or a training gap. It’s a design gap, on both sides of the wall.

What I do

I design the conditions under which your teams trust and build with AI well, and the conditions under which your customers trust and adopt what those teams ship. I treat both as one connected experience, and I bring the evidence to prove it’s working. I’m accountable for trust and adoption, internal and external, not for delivering a program. That’s the work the scaled firms structurally can’t do.

What you get

One embedded advisor working alongside your leaders, not a team deployed to run a program. Three things come out of it:

AI your customers trust. I design the moments where a person decides whether to rely on what you’ve built, so adoption and retention follow instead of stalling.

Teams equipped to build it. I set the conditions and the judgment your design, product, and engineering people need to make AI worth trusting, so quality starts upstream, not in cleanup.

Proof it’s working. I build trust on purpose and measure it, grounded in how people actually decide, so you can see the return instead of hoping for it.
