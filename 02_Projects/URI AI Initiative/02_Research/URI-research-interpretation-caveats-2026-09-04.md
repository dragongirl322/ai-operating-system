# URI research — interpretation caveats

Notes from reading the URI research (4 Sep 2026). Be careful how we use these results.

## 1. Respondent mix is heavily IC / vertical

The majority of respondents, by a long shot, are individual contributors in roles that are more vertical: application developer, AI developer, software developer, data scientist, and product manager.

In practice — especially in engineering — people in those roles often do not know what operational processes exist outside their own silo. Example: ask a software developer at Workday whether we put AI in front of end users, and they will often say no, because they are not exposed enough to what happens once their work is done, or to ongoing collaboration among cross-functional leadership.

**Implication:** Do not over-read survey answers as a picture of how the whole org actually runs AI with end users. The sample is skewed toward people who may not see that layer.

## 2. “Who owns AI evaluation” and the 3% outside-evaluators figure

On who owns AI evaluation for the initiative / share of teams: it makes sense that when respondents are mostly engineers, they say the team does its own evaluations. Most engineering teams will answer that way and disregard that other people in the company might also evaluate.

Be careful reading too much into survey emphasis on only **3%** bringing in outside evaluators. That figure mainly says that **3% pay an external vendor like URI** to do the work. It does **not** mean other people inside the company are not doing evaluation work.

Also: these respondents often would not know whether external vendors are brought in at all.

**Implication:** Do not treat “3% outside evaluators” as evidence that almost no one else is evaluating. It is a vendor-spend signal from a sample that may not see the full evaluation landscape.
