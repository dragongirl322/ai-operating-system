# URI Dynamics — Summary of Strengths, Risks & Recommendations

*Synthesized from [[URI dynamics]] (raw source notes, Ken B., Dan/Riley/Trinh, Trinh, Dalien/Justin pattern, Brooke/Shawn, Jeff — Sep 2026) plus direct observation.*

---

## The throughline

Almost every issue below traces back to one root cause: **Trinh is the leader in trust and respect, but not yet the leader in practice.** She is universally described as capable, caring, and the person the team wants in charge — and just as consistently described as invisible in the role ("nobody knows that Trinh is CEO"), avoidant of hard conversations ("doesn't have a mean bone in her body"), and not fully in command of what her own leadership team (Justin, Dalien) is doing. Until Trinh fully **owns the CEO role** — visibly, decisively, and with real authority over accountability — the alignment, transparency, and accountability gaps below will keep regenerating no matter how many individual fixes are applied.

---

## Key Strengths

1. **A trusted, respected leader.** Every source — Ken, Dan, Riley, Brooke, Shawn — independently expresses confidence in and affection for Trinh. This is rare and valuable capital; people *want* to follow her.
2. **Stable, shared core values.** Trinh names three things that "can't change": focus on quality, helping clients succeed, and caring about the team. Multiple sources independently confirm quality is genuinely strong.
3. **Buy-in for transformation.** Dan, Riley, and Trinh all confirm the company knows it needs to change and has high confidence in the help being offered — a real, current window to act.
4. **Strong core client(s) and delivery talent.** Google is a large, presumably durable anchor client. Researchers themselves are described as good, with a deep bench (14 staff, 30+ in placement).
5. **Pockets of forward motion already exist.** Brooke's ops team is ahead of the rest of the org on applied AI; Shawn is motivated to fix GTM; Trinh has already taken first steps (moving Sales to Shawn effective Nov 1) and is scoping a real strategy process through Brooke.
6. **Engaged people who see the problems clearly.** Ken, Dalien's peers, Brooke, Shawn, and Jeff all articulate the same gaps in alignment, accountability, and transparency independently and unprompted — this is a workforce that cares enough to notice and name what's wrong, which is a strength to build on, not just a risk signal.

---

## Key Risks

### 1. Unclear ownership and decision rights (structural)
People see the lack of alignment, transparency, and accountability as a defining feature of how URI runs. Concretely:
- **It's often unclear who is the decision maker.** Trinh is CEO in title, but "nobody knows that," and Justin and Dalien continue to run Innovation and AI work with minimal visibility to her.
- Ken B. describes URI as simultaneously "a cohesive unit toward a single vision" and *not* aligned on who is accountable for what — a sign the misalignment is normalized enough that people hold both views at once.
- The vision itself is "too flowery" and not concrete enough to rally around, which makes accountability hard to define in the first place — you can't hold people to a target that isn't specific.

### 2. Transparency breakdowns, especially around Justin/Innovation
- Dalien and Justin operate "in a vacuum" — feedback given (including a formal evaluation rubric) goes unacknowledged and unactioned.
- Trinh herself only learns what Justin's org is doing indirectly, "casually," and has already had to claw back Sales as a partial response.
- The Friday AI initiative meeting has no clear objective per its own regular attendee (Brooke); it's described as hard to follow and low-value — a symptom of the same pattern at the initiative level.

### 3. Accountability gaps
- No clear ownership of selling — a direct threat to revenue.
- Underperforming research managers (Tom "just riding it out," Janet described as "horrible") persist without a stated consequence or plan.
- Complacency named specifically around Tom and Ken.
- Trinh's own conflict-avoidant style ("will say 'I'm going to be mean' but can't be") makes her structurally unlikely to close these gaps without support.

### 4. Founder/authority conflict (Justin)
Justin is described as a "disruptor" and "maverick" — fast, urgent, toxically optimistic — running Innovation (including Panel Pro and Dalien's AI work) with limited accountability to Trinh. This is both a transparency risk and a direct constraint on Trinh's ability to act as CEO in practice, not just in title.

### 5. Commercial concentration and exposure
- Heavy reliance on too few clients (Google as the anchor, Meta "fickle," Amazon low-value) — flagged independently by both Trinh and Jeff.
- No real marketing/GTM engine: only two people (Justin and David) can even edit the website, which is the only marketing asset that exists.

### 6. Panel Pro as a sustained financial drag
Seven years and ~$400K/year in staff cost with no return, called out independently by both Brooke and Shawn as a business risk — a rare point of unprompted agreement that hasn't yet translated into a decision.

### 7. AI initiative lacking a clear purpose
The initiative Dalien leads is confusing even to a regular attendee, has no stated objective, and is gated in a way that keeps the researchers who'd benefit most (per Shawn) from getting access. Brooke's instinct — that AI should be core to how URI works rather than "the next big thing" — is a useful diagnostic the initiative currently fails.

### 8. Talent retention exposure
One of the best researchers is paid just over $100K, called out as unsustainable — a flight risk sitting inside the most lucrative part of the business (Research).

### 9. Loss of focus and adaptability
Brooke describes the business as trying to do too many things without a clear why, and as having lost the "scrappy adaptability" that used to define it — both downstream symptoms of the missing concrete vision.

### 10. Unresolved operational ownership
Ops work scattered across Brooke/Trinh/Justin since the prior ops head departed; Trinh is reportedly having a hard time fully letting go even as Brooke absorbs more scope — a smaller-scale echo of the same "who actually owns this" pattern.

## Recommendations

### Make Trinh CEO in practice, not just in title — this is the precondition for everything else
1. **Explicitly and visibly establish Trinh's decision authority** across all five functions (Research, Recruiting, Operations, Sales & Marketing, Innovation). Communicate it to the whole org, not just leadership — the fact that "nobody knows Trinh is CEO" is itself the finding to reverse.
2. **Get Trinh direct support in shifting her leadership style** — from conflict-avoidant and consensus-seeking toward decisive and accountability-holding. This is likely coaching or close hands-on partnership (not a one-time conversation), since her team explicitly wants her to "stop being so nice" while still trusting her completely.
3. **Resolve Justin's role now, deliberately.** Find him a lane (advisory, founder/ambassador, a bounded piece of GTM or innovation) that preserves his dignity and founder status but requires him to report into Trinh's structure with real transparency — no more "off doing his own rodeo." Do the same for Dalien's reporting line.

### Close the transparency and accountability gaps
4. **Install a standing reporting rhythm** from every function lead to Trinh (e.g., biweekly, with actual decisions/deliverables logged) — replace "working in a vacuum" with a default of visibility.
5. **Give the AI initiative a written charter**: a single clear objective, a decision-maker, and a defined audience/purpose for the Friday meeting — or fold/cancel it until one exists. Fix Brooke's gatekeeping so researchers get tool access; make AI-as-core-practice the explicit goal rather than "the next big thing."
6. **Name explicit owners for the accountability gaps already surfaced**: someone accountable for selling; a performance decision (support, reassign, or exit) for the underperforming research managers; a concrete plan addressing complacency where named.

### Sharpen strategic focus
7. **Rewrite the vision to be concrete and short enough to rally around** — this is already planned work (Trinh + your support) and should land before or at the Nov 1 kickoff, since focus and accountability both depend on having a real target.
8. **Force a decision on Panel Pro** — a time-boxed ROI review (owner, deadline) ending in sunset, restructure, or recommit-with-targets. Seven years of drift on a $400K/year cost that two independent leaders flag unprompted is a decision, not an ongoing question.
9. **Tie every active initiative back to the sharpened vision**; cut or pause what doesn't ladder up, directly addressing the "too many things, unclear why" feedback.

### Reduce commercial exposure
10. **Set an explicit client-diversification target** to reduce reliance on Google/Meta/Amazon concentration.
11. **Stand up real marketing/GTM capability** beyond two people editing a website — Shawn has already signaled intent here; give him a mandate and resourcing.
12. **Address top-researcher compensation** in the highest-margin part of the business before it becomes a retention loss.

### Use Nov 1 as the forcing function
13. Anchor the CEO-authority clarity, the sharpened vision, and the Justin role resolution to the **Nov 1 transformation kickoff** — announce them together so the org experiences one coherent shift, not a series of disconnected changes.
