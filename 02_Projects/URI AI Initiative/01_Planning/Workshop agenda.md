## 1. Kickoff — context and ground rules

### Why transform

URI has been successful for 20+ years — and the game has changed. Research budgets have shrunk. AI can take on some of the research lift. In tech, there is a perception that AI can replace us. Those shifts require a transformative approach to drive focus and growth.

### What we preserve

Change is necessary. So is protecting what makes us great. The foundation is solid: great research, high quality, a robust panel, and capable people.

### How we lead the work

Jim Collins and other leadership thinkers are clear: a leader’s job is to catalyze a clear vision and strategy. We build a culture of supportive development and growth, and we use well-founded change-management norms — not disruption for its own sake.

### Today’s goal

Create a vision using the **Collins/Porras Vision Framework**.

Jim Collins and Jerry Porras studied enduring visionary companies (Built to Last) and distilled how those firms define vision with rigor: **core ideology** (core values + core purpose) and an **envisioned future** (BHAG / mission + vivid description). The framework is valuable because it separates what must never change from what must change — and the research evidence shows this pattern in companies that endure.

### Ground rules

- Be present.
- Listen to each other before interjecting.
- Leave today with: **core values**, **core purpose**, and **mission (BHAG)**.
- Decisions by consensus — everyone bought in. If we cannot reach consensus, **Trinh is the tie-breaker**.

## Discussion weave-ins

Notes to fold into the conversation once we know where they fit. Not separate homework.

### Tightening what we mean by innovation

URI has an innovation objective, but we risk treating “innovation” as synonymous with building tools. That is too narrow for a research firm — and it puts capital and attention into the hardest, slowest path by default.

Propose we simplify innovation into **three buckets**:

1. **GTM** — How we find, win, and grow the right clients. New packaging, buyer paths, demand creation, and engagement models that deepen strategic relationships (not random market hopping).

2. **Services** — What we offer and how we deliver it. New or redesigned offerings, methods, and delivery models that strengthen quality, client success, and team capacity.

3. **Tooling** — Software and platforms we build. Only when there is a clear opportunity — and **start inward**: use tooling to solve our own operating challenges (quality, speed, consistency, margin) before we build products to sell.

**Working rules for the room**

- All three count as innovation. Tooling is one vector, not the definition.
- Sequence bias: GTM and Services first when the question is “how do we move enterprise value in the next 18–24 months?”
- Tooling filter: If it does not clearly improve how URI operates (or unlock a service we already know how to sell), it waits.
- Non-negotiables still apply: quality, helping clients succeed, caring for the team. Any innovation idea that weakens those is out.

**Groundwork (from Collins — [Can a Hedgehog be a Program?](https://www.jimcollins.com/media_topics/CanAHedgehogBeAProgram.html))**

A Hedgehog Concept is not a program you stand up in a few months. In the good-to-great research it took **four years on average** to get the concept clear, and **seven years on average** from the start of the transition to a visible breakthrough. Looking back, the answer looks simple and elegant — so people assume they can “programmatize” it quickly — but it actually comes through years of debate, angst, and evolution. Lay that expectation before we commit to defining ours later.
