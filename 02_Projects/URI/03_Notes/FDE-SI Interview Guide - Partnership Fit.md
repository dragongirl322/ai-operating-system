# FDE/SI Interview Guide: Partnership Fit

**Goal:** learn whether a partnership makes sense, how it would work, and at which stage of their engagements. Spend most of the time listening. Hold any URI pitch until the end, and only if it's invited.

**Open question:** will Shawn assign a researcher to help run these interviews? Follow up after the Shawn 1:1.

## Opening (2 min)
"Thanks for making time. I'm trying to understand how firms like yours run AI implementations from start to finish, where things go well, and where they get hard. There are no right answers, and I'll keep specifics about your clients confidential."

## 1. How deals start (who buys and what they sign)
- How do you set up contracts with your clients? *Probe: fixed scope or time and materials, pilot then expansion, who writes the SOW.*
- Who is your primary buyer? *Probe: their title, who else has to sign off, and who owns the budget.*
- What are you usually contractually bound to deliver? *Probe: outputs or outcomes, and whether adoption or user success is ever in the contract.*

*Partnership signal:* Whether URI's work could live inside their SOW, run alongside it, or come before it.

## 2. Discovery and requirements
- When clients come to you, how much do they typically know about what they want and need?
- How do you gather requirements?
- What details are you looking for as you decide what to build? *Probe: workflows, data, decision points, and how much autonomy the AI should have.*
- What role, if any, do the client's end users play in setting requirements? *Probe: when you talk to them, how often, and who represents them.*

*Partnership signal:* How much they rely on assumptions about users before they build. That gap is where a pre-build assessment fits.

## 3. Building and validating
- Once you start building, how do you check that the solution is going to work? *Probe: who tests it, real users or proxies, and what counts as passing.*
- What needs to be true before you go into production? *Probe: is there a formal gate, and who signs off on it?*

*Partnership signal:* Whether there's a natural checkpoint before production that independent findings could inform.

## 4. After launch
- Once a solution is deployed, what do you track?
- What are your success metrics, and how do you track them? *Probe: usage or adoption, trust, error and override rates, and who sees the results.*

*Partnership signal:* Whether anyone measures if people actually use the system and want to, and who would pay to find out.

## 5. Pain points
- What are the biggest pain points in implementation work? *Probe: ask for a recent example, what it cost them, and what they wish they had known earlier.*
- When a project struggles after launch, what's usually behind it?

## 6. Partnership (last 5 minutes)
- Do you bring in outside specialists today? For what, and how is that set up?
- If someone could tell you before launch whether real users would trust and adopt the system, at what point would that help most?
- Would you rather resell that work, refer clients to it, or bring it in for joint pursuits?

## Closing
"What should I have asked you that I didn't? Who else should I talk to?"

## After the interview, note
- Buyer title
- Contract model
- How involved users are (none, some, or a lot)
- Whether there's a pre-production gate
- Whether adoption is tracked
- Top pain point
- Best entry stage for a partnership (discovery, validation, pre-production, or post-launch)
