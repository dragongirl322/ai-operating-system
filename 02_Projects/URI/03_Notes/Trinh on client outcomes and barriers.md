# Trinh — client outcomes, tools, and barriers

Captured 8 Sep 2026. Tam's questions and Trinh's response. Raw, for future reference.

## Questions Tam asked

What are the key outcomes their clients are expected to deliver that would benefit from research, and what are the things making that hard? What are the tools and processes they are using today, and how well are those tools and processes helping them do the job?

An alternative view: from URI's perspective, what is preventing clients from engaging with and leveraging URI? Do we have an opportunity to break down those barriers?

## Trinh's response

On key outcomes, what URI is increasingly hearing from clients is a desire for insights that are not only delivered more quickly, but are also more strategic and actionable. Historically URI has been known as strong executors of research, but the expectation has evolved. Clients are looking for URI to bring a stronger thought leadership perspective: helping them understand not just what is happening, but why it matters and what actions they should take as a result.

Another area where URI can create significant value is benchmarking metrics and longitudinal dashboards. Trinh thinks Tam's AI Metrics can be great to measure here. Many organizations repeatedly conduct similar research studies over time, but insights often remain fragmented as team members transition in and out. Limited institutional knowledge, no centralized view of historical findings, and little ability to measure progress against previous research. A systematic approach to capturing, benchmarking, and tracking insights over time would help clients build a more durable knowledge base, demonstrate the impact of changes made, and make better informed decisions based on evidence rather than isolated studies. Having a panel that can access their profile participants quickly and build on their profile could help.

On barriers to engaging with and leveraging URI: URI needs to transform how it approaches research and understand where it can fill the gap when it comes to user data and the information clients need to influence product decisions. The question, as Trinh is thinking about it: how to convert massive volumes of fragmented user data into trustworthy, decision-ready insights.
