## What we have learned about the business

URI is a long-standing user research firm under pressure to become a clearer, more saleable growth company. Trinh cares deeply about a strong outcome in roughly the next 18 to 24 months. The firm needs transformation, not cosmetic AI projects.

**Clients and concentration.** Clients want faster, more strategic, actionable insight and thought leadership, not only research execution. Google is the largest client. Amazon is thin and price-sensitive. Heavy reliance on too few clients is a real risk. Jeff separately named client diversification as a challenge.

**Org and accountability.** Research is the profitable core. Sales and marketing capability is widely seen as weak. Not enough people are accountable for selling. Innovation (including Panel Pro and Dalien’s AI work) has sat under Justin with low transparency to Trinh and the rest of the company. Sales is moving to Shawn (targeted for November 1, alongside FY27 strategy rollout). Ken, Brooke, Shawn, and Jeff converge on the same themes: unclear accountability, lost focus, and a vision that is not concrete enough for people to work toward. Trinh herself called the vision too flowery.

**Vision and rollout.** On September 28, Tam facilitated a successful vision-setting workshop in which the leadership group selected URI’s core values, core purpose, and BHAG mission. Brooke owns finalizing the language and aligning company goals to the mission. Trinh has asked Tam to define a rollout plan beginning November 1. This advances the vision issue from diagnosis to an initial intervention; its effectiveness will depend on final language, explicit priorities and tradeoffs, aligned goals, owners, and sustained leadership behavior.

**CEO authority and founder transition.** Dan named Trinh CEO because he trusts and respects her and has told Tam he would support Trinh asking Justin to leave if she judged that necessary. Trinh rejected Justin’s proposal that they serve as co-CEOs because she believes two-headed leadership will not work. The broader team sees Trinh—not Justin—as the leader and wants her to lean further into the role. The unresolved issue is not whether she has legitimacy, capability, or owner backing; it is whether she will consistently enforce CEO authority when doing so creates conflict with Justin. Justin is reported to bypass her, and she knows this occurs but has not consistently confronted it.

**Working diagnosis — early and testable.** URI is experiencing an incomplete founder-to-CEO operating transition. Formal authority, investor backing, team legitimacy, and CEO capability are present. The central constraint is inconsistent enforcement of CEO authority where decisions affect Justin’s identity, status, or historic influence. Trinh has described the situation as hard: she feels gratitude and personal obligation to Justin, does not want to undermine his self-esteem as founder, acknowledges that her own lack of action is a problem, and has asked Tam to hold her accountable. This dynamic appears to reinforce fragmented ownership, hidden work, and weak accountability. This week provides mixed evidence: Trinh authorized the readiness kickoff and made an explicit decision to allow the two AI efforts to remain separate for a bounded period, rather than leaving parallel work unacknowledged; she also says ownership will ultimately consolidate under Tam regardless of the Evals outcome. The diagnosis remains under test because the review date, success criteria, integration mechanism, and response to future bypassing are not yet explicit. A plausible but unproven hypothesis is that conditions may become more disruptive temporarily as Justin’s informal authority is constrained.

**What must not change.** Quality, helping clients succeed, and caring about the team.

**Justin and AI execution.** Alignment between Trinh and Justin is weak. Justin and Dalien have often operated with little visible progress or transparency. Tam has directly told both that she has low confidence in the current HITL Eval effort. Tam has now led the kickoff for the Human–AI Readiness Assurance effort with Trinh, Shawn, and Lingan. Trinh intends to give Justin six more weeks to deliver on the Evals effort because he has three client meetings lined up, and she and Tam have agreed that the efforts can remain separate during that period. Trinh says that regardless of the Evals outcome, the work will ultimately roll into execution led by Tam; if Dalien is not the right person for that execution, URI will replace him. The decision is now a time-bounded evidence window rather than an immediate shutdown, but it still needs a precise review date, success criteria, rules for avoiding conflict, and a defined consolidation decision. Panel Pro is also repeatedly named as a business risk, with the dynamics notes estimating roughly seven years of investment and about $400K per year in staff cost without demonstrated return.

**Talent and management.** Early interviews surface material performance-management and retention risks. Shawn is described as performing well in important areas but not addressing poor performance. Two research managers are characterized by colleagues as serious performance problems; those reports still need to be translated into observable behavior, prior feedback, business impact, and documented expectations before conclusions are reached. The recruiting manager is seen as effective leading her team but less effective helping the broader business. One strong researcher’s compensation has also been flagged as a possible retention risk. These are signals requiring evidence and accountable management action, not yet settled judgments about individuals.

**Leadership-trust watchpoint — Shawn.** Shawn is clearly sharp and is becoming important to URI’s growth agenda. He told Tam that Trinh sometimes gets close to acting on a decision she has shared with him and then backs out, almost always when Justin is involved. He also said he was supposed to receive a $60,000 equity bonus last year, did not receive it, and was never told why. These are Shawn’s reports and have not been independently verified. Tam’s concern that accumulated disappointment could become bitterness or subtle undermining is a hypothesis, not an observed conclusion. The immediate evidence need is to watch for concrete behavior, understand Trinh’s account, and avoid treating either resentment or undermining as established fact.

**The AI / GTM strategic truth.** Competing broadly in HITL evaluation labor puts URI against Scale, Mercor, Surge, and similar labs that own frontier-lab spend. URI’s survey signals an evaluation gap, but about 3% outside-evaluator use does not prove willingness to pay for that SKU. More importantly, URI’s natural buyer is typically a UX or research leader. Serious model-eval and HITL buyers are usually CTO, Chief AI Officer, or VP Product. Chasing generic HITL means rebuilding GTM muscle URI does not currently have.

**The sharper bet.** Own independent human–AI readiness assurance for consequential mid-market B2B workflows (finance, insurance, healthcare), not a menu of HITL modules. AI should be core to growth strategy, not a separate lab or side initiative. Weakest evidence gap remains willingness to pay for that exact readiness offer.

## How Tam has helped shape the future

Tam has helped move the emerging direction from a diffuse “do AI stuff / sell HITL catalog” story toward a more coherent commercial and leadership thesis. This remains proposed direction rather than an executed transformation.

**Diagnosis.** She built a living dynamics picture (Ken, Trinh, Brooke, Shawn, Jeff, Dan/Riley) so leadership conversations rest on pattern, not anecdote. She named the accountability, vision, concentration, and Justin-opacity problems early.

**Strategy and offer.** She challenged weak brochure and research framing, filed interpretation caveats, and drove the pivot: stop competing as generic HITL labor; own human–AI workflow readiness; educate the market; prove with pilots and partners before a platform bet. She framed AI as the growth engine and innovation as concrete buckets (GTM, services, tooling inward-first; Five Lenses IP; panel intelligence; partners; Research Hub), not a vague AI lab.

**Leadership and change.** She prepared Dan and Trinh conversations with evidence, talking points that preserve Justin’s dignity while still creating room for a new mandate, and decision questions. She brought Collins/Porras into the vision workshop (preserve the core / stimulate progress), successfully facilitated the group’s selection of core values, core purpose, and BHAG mission, and has now been asked to define the November 1 rollout. She drafted GTM strategy for Trinh’s team and a possible Tam–Shawn path around the November sales handoff. Justin’s future lane remains unresolved and must be decided by Trinh rather than treated as settled in the advisory work.

**Role clarity.** She refused to be a side dish on an opaque Justin/Dalien track. She packaged her seat as **Executive Advisor, Strategy & Growth** (Advisor to the CEO): strategy, integrated growth including AI, client relations, and leadership mentoring, with clear decision rights and PE-recognizable value-creation outcomes. No “fractional” language.

**Title framing:** Executive Advisor, Strategy & Growth. Advisor to the CEO (Trinh).

**Purpose:** Increase strategic clarity, growth quality, and execution capacity. Define where URI will play and how it will win, then help the company follow through. AI is core to that growth agenda, not a separate entity.

**Primary work:** CEO counsel (responsive and proactive); strategy definition and execution; integrated growth (retention/expansion, new business, GTM, AI positioning); selective client relations; leadership mentoring and facilitation when they serve strategy.

## Through-line

URI has now selected the core of a more concrete direction; the next challenge is turning it into goals, choices, ownership, and visible behavior beginning November 1. The leading diagnosis remains an incomplete founder-to-CEO operating transition: Trinh has the formal authority, owner backing, team legitimacy, and demonstrated capability to lead, but has not yet consistently enforced that authority where Justin is concerned. Her explicit six-week Evals window is a clearer decision than indefinite parallel work, but its value depends on defined evidence and follow-through. Human–AI Readiness Assurance remains the leading AI growth hypothesis, not yet validated demand. Sales accountability, client diversification, talent management, leadership trust, and cross-functional visibility also need to improve. Tam’s value is as the CEO’s strategy and growth advisor who helps make commitments, decision rights, evidence, and accountability explicit—without becoming a substitute CEO or operator for work internal leaders must own.
