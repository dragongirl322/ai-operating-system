## Scene 1: Dinner After Leah

### Beat 1: Jack Wants To Eat

Jack and Simone go out for dinner after his appointment. They sit in a booth beside a window. Jack is tired and emotionally raw, but his returning appetite is a small, concrete sign that the session helped. He is glad to be with Simone and does not need to explain everything immediately.

**Jack's internal shift:** For a brief period, he feels present in his adult life. Wanting food and Simone's company feels ordinary in a way his body has not allowed for several days.

**Story function:** Carries the earned hope from the therapy close into the world outside Leah's office. The memory emerges from increased openness, not from another therapeutic prompt.

### Beat 2: The Honda Arrives

Jack hears a motorcycle approaching before he sees it. A Honda like Frank's pulls into view outside the restaurant window. The sound, shape, and movement of the bike capture his attention so completely that the restaurant begins to fall away.

**Jack's internal shift:** Recognition arrives before explanation. His body knows the motorcycle as something loved and dangerous.

**Story function:** Provides a specific sensory bridge into memory and reinforces that Jack's body carries associations his conscious mind has not organized.

### Beat 3: The Good Father Comes First

The first image is Frank taking young Jack for a ride on his Honda. Jack is small enough that holding on requires his whole body. He remembers the engine's vibration, the speed, and the thrill of being the child Frank has chosen to take with him.

Fragments of other good memories can surface around the ride: Frank teaching Jack to steer his trike down the driveway, helping him when he gets stuck, and teaching him to fish.

**Jack's internal shift:** He does not begin by remembering Frank as a monster. He remembers wanting his father's attention and feeling special when he received it.
