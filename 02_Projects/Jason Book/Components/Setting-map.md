## Boarding School

**Name (locked):** St. Aidan's School (for Boys)

**Location (town / state):** Juniper Springs, Oregon — high desert east of the Cascades, roughly between Bend and Burns on the Highway 20 corridor. Isolated; nearest real town ~40 minutes away.

**Founded:** 1947, by Episcopal Bishop Corwin Whitfield and a rancher-benefactor (wartime lumber money). Publicly framed as a charitable mission for boys who "need direction" — the language families use instead of admitting they want a son out of sight. Still open and operating as of the present day; nothing dramatic happened to it that Jack would ever have read about.

**Character:** Episcopal-affiliated, chapel-centered — mandatory chapel twice daily, a merit/demerit ledger, older boys ("prefects") enforcing order on younger ones. Discipline dressed as devotion; control offered as care. That framing deliberately echoes Mona's "because I loved you" — keep the resonance close to the surface without naming it on the page.

**Campus texture:** Stone-and-timber main hall (partly built by student labor, a fact the school still brags about); dorms named for saints (Cuthbert House, Chad House); a chapel bell tower audible across the flat scrubland; a 1962 brick building known unofficially as **the Annex**, used for boys in isolation.

**Headmaster (Rector) in Jack's time:** The Rev. Elliot Marsh, in post since 1961 — never raises his voice, remembers birthdays, quotes scripture gently. More frightening than a shouter because no single moment of his is ever cruel.

**Who gets sent there:** Ranching/timber families across the Pacific Northwest, some military kids (Klamath Falls, Umatilla area), and — quietly — boys like Jack, sent by families who need the problem to become invisible rather than solved.

**Jack's belief:** "where they send bad kids like me" (delinquent warehouse).

**Story truth under that belief:** Real science / art / music programs; specific teachers who notice him — not only punishment. The institution's cold, disciplinary architecture (chapel, ledger, prefects, the Annex) is genuinely there, but it coexists with pockets of real attention Jack received. That contradiction is deliberate: the school shouldn't read as pure gothic cruelty — the horror is procedural and quiet, and the good parts are real, which is part of why Jack's "thrown away" narrative is a misfiling, not a lie.

**Household at the send-off (locked):** Before summer 1985 home visit. Mona already with James Price. Who stays home: Mona, James, Stephen, baby Owen. Frank not in that household.

**Timing:** Boarding school is Phase 1 memory — before the summer 1985 visit / murder (Phase 3).
