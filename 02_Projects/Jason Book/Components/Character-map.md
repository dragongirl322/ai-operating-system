# Jason Book Character Map

Working character map for the mystery thriller centered on Jack Tate, Stephen Tate, buried memory, family narrative control, and the cost of silence.

## Core Story Argument

Jack's silence was never his safety. It was Stephen's alibi.

The story argues that survival becomes freedom only when the truth once buried for safety is reclaimed, spoken, and used to protect someone else.

The opposing force argues that forgetting is mercy, silence is loyalty, and the past only has power if someone insists on naming it.

## Character Web

### Jack Tate

**Role:** Protagonist.

**Age:** 48.

**Core identity:** Former Seattle musician whose body has begun breaking under the pressure of memory he cannot consciously hold.

**Backstory:** Jack survived childhood abuse, time in and out of foster care, and the public suspicion that he raped and killed an older female classmate as a teenager. He has always insisted he did not kill her, but his own memory of the night is incomplete and distorted by trauma.

**Adult life:** Jack was part of a well-known Seattle band that blended 70s/80s hair rock with Seattle grunge influences. The band released four LPs, and one LP had a couple of songs that reached the top 100 most streamed. Jack wrote songs, played guitar, and performed backup vocals. Until three years ago, he made an okay living through music. Since then, panic attacks, headaches, stomach issues, and other symptoms have disrupted his life. He now works odd jobs and is financially dependent, in part, on his live-in girlfriend, Simone Avery.

**Want:** To regain enough stability to live without shame, return to music, and stop feeling like a burden.

**Need:** To accept that his body is not betraying him; it is carrying testimony. He must stop organizing his life around not knowing.

**Fear:** That remembering will destroy him, prove he is dangerous, or reveal that the family and town were right about him.

**Gift:** Music. His guitar is not just a profession or talent; it is the part of him that still knows how to regulate, feel, connect, and tell the truth without words.

**Wound:** The family system taught him that he is the damaged one, the unreliable one, the problem, the failure, and the person whose needs make others unsafe.

**Voice and body compass:** Jack narrates like a man who can feel every wrong note in a room, but has been trained to believe the wrongness is him. He registers threat somatically before he can consciously interpret it: shaking hands, nausea, pressure, heat, headache, shame, the need to leave, the need to apologize, the urge to smoke, or the urge to make a joke.

**Protective pattern:** Jack is fiercely protective of vulnerable people but almost never includes himself among them. He protects Tyler's confidence, Megan from judgment, Simone from the full weight of his fear, Mona from guilt, and even Owen from accountability because Owen does not fully know what he is echoing. Around Stephen, this protective instinct gets inverted: Jack does not consciously protect Stephen as a person, but he protects the story that keeps Stephen credible and himself suspect.

**Symbolic function:** The body as witness. The designated damage. The person whose silence has protected everyone except himself.

**Relationship to theme:** Jack embodies the theme under pressure. His arc moves from silence-as-survival to truth-as-rescue.

**Opening image possibility:** Jack hears Tyler Holt playing guitar in a neighbor's garage. He encourages the boy, briefly plays something beautiful, then sees Tyler's aliveness and vulnerability. Jack's hand begins to shake. Stephen's car arrives, interrupting the moment.

### Frank Stephen Tate

**Role:** Jack and Stephen's father; original family regime.

**Status:** Deceased four years before the present.

**Core identity:** Blue-collar successful businessman who ran a small construction business and treated work, toughness, and control as measures of human worth.

**Family position:** Frank had been estranged from Jack for several years before his death but remained close to Stephen. He favored Stephen, taught him how to dominate, humiliate, and control, and made Stephen feel like the rightful inheritor of the family's authority.

**Relationship to Jack:** Frank was Jack's primary overt abuser. He taught Jack that power meant danger, home meant surveillance, and needing protection could make things worse.

**Relationship to Stephen:** Stephen carries Frank's middle name and inherits the family posture Frank modeled: authority as competence, cruelty as discipline, control as responsibility, and weakness as failure.

**Want:** Obedience, control, and a family hierarchy that confirmed his own strength.

**Fear:** Weakness, exposure, loss of authority, and anything that challenged his version of manhood.

**Symbolic function:** The original regime. Frank built houses and broke people; Stephen inherits the name, the posture, and the permission structure.

**Relationship to theme:** Frank is the source system that made silence feel like survival. His abuse created the family logic Stephen later weaponizes into alibi.

### Stephen Tate

**Role:** Antagonist. Jack's older brother.

**Age:** 50.

**Core identity:** Polished, charismatic, successful businessman who inherited the family system's authority and uses respectability as cover.

**Backstory:** Stephen participated in abusing Jack as a child. Their father favored Stephen and taught him how to dominate, humiliate, and control. Stephen is also the actual killer in the old murder case and raped Jack.

**Adult life:** Stephen owns three successful used car lots in the greater Seattle area. He opened his first lot in 2005 and has built a strong local reputation. He has never married or had children and is a bit of a player. He donates generously to local charities and promotes that giving both as part of his business and as proof of his character.

**Public mask:** Steady, controlled, generous, successful, civic-minded, charming, and responsible.

**Private method:** Narrative control. He makes himself look like the reasonable caretaker and makes Jack look unstable, dependent, jealous, obsessive, or dangerous.

**Want:** To preserve his status, maintain control of the family story, keep Jack contained, and prevent anything from disturbing the old case.

**Surface justification:** He says he wants Jack to get his act together, stop being a burden, and stop embarrassing the family.

**Deeper motive:** Jack becoming functional, believed, loved, musically alive, or curious threatens Stephen. Jack's instability protects Stephen because it keeps Jack easy to dismiss.

**Fear:** Exposure. Loss of control. Jack being believed. Mona remembering something. Simone seeing the system too clearly.

**Symbolic function:** The alibi. The inheritor of the father's regime. Respectability as predation.

**Relationship to theme:** Stephen personifies the counter-theme: silence is loyalty, forgetting is mercy, and the family survives by burying what happened.

**Typical pressure language:**

- "I'm worried about him."
- "He's not in a good place."
- "You know how he gets."
- "Mom doesn't need to be managing that."
- "He's always had a hard time letting things go."
- "I'm not saying he'd hurt anyone. I'm saying he's not himself right now."

### Simone Avery

**Role:** Jack's girlfriend, live-in partner, and possible mentor figure.

**Core identity:** Practical, analytical, emotionally perceptive woman who sees Jack's strength more clearly than he does.

**Work:** Office manager at a small law firm.

**Education and capacity:** College dropout and polymath. She once studied psychology with hopes of becoming a therapist, but left school to care for her dying mother.

**What she sees in Jack:** Brilliance, kindness, capability, protectiveness, and a damaged but still intact moral center.

**Want:** To help Jack stabilize without reducing him to his symptoms.

**Need:** To learn how to challenge Jack's certainty without making him feel disbelieved, and how to witness the truth without trying to rescue him from the work only he can do.

**Fear:** That Jack will disappear into shame, symptoms, or self-destruction before he can reclaim himself.

**Symbolic function:** The outside witness. Pattern recognition. Love that believes him but does not let him hide inside incomplete certainty.

**Relationship to theme:** Simone helps Jack move from "I must not remember" to "what my body knows deserves to be heard."

**Important line energy:** "I believe you did not kill her. But I do not think you remember that night as clearly as you think you do."

### Leah Moreno

**Role:** Jack's licensed therapist, accessed through social services because Jack no longer has steady income.

**Age and presence:** Older than Jack, younger than Mona. Grounded, practical, and emotionally steady. She is very different from Mona: clear where Mona is manipulative, boundaried where Mona is needy, and direct without cruelty.

**Therapeutic function:** Leah helps Jack stay with memory without drowning in it. She does not solve the mystery, diagnose the plot, or overinterpret clues. She notices patterns, asks precise questions, and helps Jack tolerate the next layer of truth.

**Relationship to Jack:** Jack has been avoiding therapy and often returns frustrated or angry when he does go. His anger is partly resistance: therapy begins to unlock the survival equation that has kept him safe, or at least alive.

**Symbolic function:** Safe witnessing. A non-family listener who can receive Jack's memories without needing them to protect the family story.

**Relationship to theme:** Leah helps Jack experience memory as something he can survive. She makes it possible for him to touch truth without immediately being destroyed by it.

### Mona Price

**Role:** Jack and Stephen's mother.

**Core identity:** Aging, physically healthy woman showing early signs of dementia; frail on the surface, sharp and cruel underneath.

**Backstory:** Mona was abused by Jack and Stephen's father and later by her second husband, James Price. She presents as weak, incapable, and dependent, but she has also caused deep harm through manipulation and cruel remarks.

**Relationship to Jack:** Mona is the one person from Jack's childhood who showed any care for him, usually through music. She influenced his taste in music and was a piano player. At the same time, she undermined Jack's sense of self and safety with covert cruelty, emotional manipulation, and cutting remarks.

**Relationship to music:** Music is the most tender part of Mona's connection to Jack. She gave him beauty without giving him safety.

**Relationship to Stephen:** Stephen remains close to Mona and has bought her a home next door to him. He controls access to her under the guise of responsible care.

**Want:** To be cared for, protected, and emotionally centered, while avoiding full accountability for what happened in the family.

**Need:** Possibly to speak the truth, even if dementia makes that truth fragmented, unreliable, or accidental.

**Fear:** Abandonment, exposure, dependence, and the collapse of the stories that have allowed her to survive.

**Symbolic function:** Corrupted care. The damaged witness. Beauty without safety.

**Relationship to theme:** Mona complicates the meaning of memory because she may remember emotionally, musically, or fragmentarily before she can state facts cleanly. Her decline makes her both vulnerable and dangerous to Stephen.

### James Price

**Role:** Mona's second husband and Jack's stepfather.

**Core identity:** Ordinary, violent man whose home briefly appeared to offer shelter and then became another site of expulsion.

**Backstory function:** After the death of the young woman and Jack's accusation, Jack had limited exposure to James. James allowed Jack to stay for one week before beating him badly after abusing Mona, burning all of Jack's clothes, and kicking him out.

**Want:** Control, dominance, and the right to define who belongs in his home.

**Need:** Not currently defined; he may function more as backstory than active present-day character unless his presence directly pressures the current plot.

**Symbolic function:** Second-household brutality. The world after accusation. Ritual rejection.

**Relationship to theme:** James reinforces the lesson that Jack is disposable and that homes are places where other people decide whether he deserves safety.

**Craft note:** Use James sparingly unless he has an active present-day function. His strongest purpose may be to explain why Jack learned that even escape routes could become violent.

### Owen Price

**Role:** Jack's younger half-brother, biological son of Mona and James Price.

**Core identity:** Mona's favorite and the family member coddled by Mona and Stephen. He is Stephen's unintentional echo.

**Personality:** A bit thick, daft, and unaware of how much harm he repeats. He can sometimes seem genuinely sympathetic toward Jack, then echo Stephen's framing and create more confusion.

**Want:** To keep family peace and remain in the comfortable role the family gave him.

**Need:** To recognize that neutrality inside a controlled family system is not neutral.

**Fear:** Conflict, disapproval, losing his favored status, or having to rethink the family story.

**Symbolic function:** The child the family chose to protect. Inherited narrative without original intent.

**Relationship to Jack:** Owen can occasionally show warmth, making Jack hope for a brotherly connection. Then he repeats Stephen's worldview without realizing it.

**Relationship to theme:** Owen shows how the alibi spreads through a family. He did not create the lie, but he benefits from it and carries it forward.

**Typical echo language:**

- "Steve's just worried about you."
- "Mom gets confused, Jack. You know that."
- "You've always had a hard time letting things go."
- "I'm not saying you're lying. I'm saying maybe you remember it wrong."
- "He's done a lot for this family."

### Megan Holt

**Role:** Jack's neighbor, close female friend, and potential living person at risk.

**Core identity:** Warm, practical single mother recovering from a years-long emotionally abusive relationship.

**Backstory:** Megan has a teenage son, Tyler. She came out of a long relationship with a man who could not hold a job because he could not stay away from alcohol. She is trying to rebuild stability for herself and her son.

**Relationship to Jack:** Megan became Jack's friend because they are neighbors. They talk frequently. Jack knows Tyler and feels protective of him.

**Vulnerability:** Stephen's stability, self-control, confidence, money, and social polish are attractive to Megan because they appear to be the opposite of what hurt her before.

**Want:** Stability, safety, adult partnership, and a better future for Tyler.

**Need:** To learn that control can masquerade as stability.

**Fear:** Repeating old patterns, failing Tyler, and being drawn toward another man whose surface hides danger.

**Symbolic function:** The living stakes. The person who makes Jack's forgetting morally dangerous.

**Relationship to theme:** Megan's risk turns memory into rescue. Jack's silence no longer affects only him.

### Tyler Holt

**Role:** Megan's teenage son; young guitarist; vulnerability trigger for Jack.

**Core identity:** A teenage boy struggling to find himself, with enough musical promise to catch Jack's attention.

**Relationship to Jack:** Tyler plays guitar in the garage. Jack likes him, encourages him, and feels protective of him.

**Opening function:** Tyler's aliveness and vulnerability expose Jack's body. When Tyler asks Jack to play guitar, Jack briefly reconnects with his gift, then sees Tyler looking at him like everything is still possible. Jack's hand begins to shake, signaling an oncoming panic attack.

**Want:** To be seen, to become someone, and to find a form for his own emerging identity.

**Need:** To be protected from adults who would use his vulnerability as access.

**Symbolic function:** The boy before damage. The reminder of what Jack lost. The future Jack may still be able to protect.

**Relationship to theme:** Tyler makes the cost of silence visible. If Jack cannot remember for himself, he may have to remember for someone like Tyler.

## Family Power Map

### Original Regime

**Frank Stephen Tate:** Overt physical terror and original authority.

**Mona Price:** Beauty plus covert cruelty; the mother who gave music but not safety.

**Stephen Tate:** Favored son and apprentice to the father's abuse; later inheritor of control.

**Jack Tate:** Designated damage, target, scapegoat, and unreliable witness.

### Second Household

**James Price:** Violence after supposed refuge; expulsion and erasure.

**Owen Price:** The child the family chose to protect and coddle.

### Present-Day System

**Stephen:** Controls the family story through money, reputation, charity, and concern.

**Mona:** Lives next door to Stephen in a home he bought; her early dementia makes her both dependent and potentially dangerous.

**Owen:** Repeats Stephen's version of reality without fully understanding its harm.

**Jack:** Experiences symptoms that the family can use as evidence that he is unstable.

**Simone:** Sees the pattern from outside the family and can name what Jack has been trained not to see.

**Megan and Tyler:** Bring the family pattern into the present by giving Jack someone living to protect.

## Coherence Tests for Future Scenes

Use these questions to keep the characters consistent:

1. Does Jack's body react before his conscious mind can explain why?
2. Does Stephen's cruelty look like concern, competence, generosity, or responsibility from the outside?
3. Does Simone believe Jack while still challenging incomplete certainty?
4. Does Mona mix tenderness and harm, especially through music, dependence, or cutting remarks?
5. Does Owen echo Stephen without fully understanding that he is doing it?
6. Does Megan's attraction to Stephen make emotional sense based on her past need for stability?
7. Does Tyler's vulnerability awaken Jack's protective instinct and grief for his own younger self?
8. Does each family interaction reinforce the old hierarchy: Stephen as credible, Jack as unstable, Mona as dependent, Owen as protected?
9. Does the mystery pressure the emotional wound instead of running beside it?
10. Does remembering become increasingly necessary to protect someone living?

## Current Opening Sequence Seed

Jack hears Tyler playing guitar in Megan's garage and stops by. He gives Tyler a sincere, specific compliment. Tyler asks Jack to play, and Jack resists before accepting the guitar. For a few moments, Jack plays beautifully. Tyler watches him with awe and openness, and Jack sees in the boy's eyes a version of aliveness and vulnerability that his own life has been organized around surviving. Jack's hand starts to shake. He sees Stephen's car arrive across the street.

Stephen later enters Jack and Simone's home and performs concern as control. He notices the lack of food and implies Jack is failing Simone. He leaves money for groceries in a way that appears generous but feels degrading. When Simone comes home, she finds Jack in a panic attack. Jack tells her it was Tyler's eyes: he looked at Jack like everything was still possible.
