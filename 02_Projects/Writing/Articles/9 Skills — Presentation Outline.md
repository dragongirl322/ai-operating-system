# 9 Skills the AI Era Demands — Presentation Outline

**Duration:** 35 min talk + 15 min Q&A
**Audience:** UX practitioners, mixed ICs + leaders
**Primary outcome:** Urgency + a starting action

**Depth map:**
- **Deep:** 1 AI Literacy · 2 Change Management · 5 Systems Thinking · 9 Storytelling
- **Medium-deep:** 6 Problem Framing
- **Light:** 3 Cross-Functional Influence · 4 Craft Mastery · 7 Business Acumen · 8 Data Literacy

**Source article:** [[9 Skills the AI Era Demands]]

---

## I. Open — Name the moment (2 min)

- **Hook:** "I've been doing this since floppy discs" Son says I'm pioneer
- Timing right; CLI -> GUI; human cognition didn't change but the tech changed what was possible and the form factor we were designing and building for
- This is a whole different thing--the capabilities are unprecedented and what makes it especially interesting is that AI can do big chunks of our job
- Our objective hasn't changed — make technology work for humans. AI raises the stakes.
- Not "is our profession dying." The question is whether we show up equipped. What are the skills that are uniquely human and important for us to have in order to meet our objectives.
- Preview: 9 skills, real time on the ones that matter most for *this moment*. You'll leave with one thing to do this week.

## II. The foundation: Mastery — plant the flag (1.5 min)

- In Dr. Sarah Lewis's book The Rise, she draws the line between success and master. 
- Success is a moment, you hit the target and it's over almost as soon as it happens.
- Mastery is never finished. It lives in the space between where you are now and the thing barely out of your reach.
- It is like the archer who keeps moving the target back on purpose. Not to fail. To stay in pursuit.
- The near win, the shot that lands just off center, is what pulls you forward.
- Don't hold these nine skills as a checklist but as targets we keep moving back as you grow. 
- None of us will master all nine, that's not the point. The point is to reach for the version of each one that sits just past what you can do today. 
- SO as we go, don't ask "can I do this." as what's the next reach.

## III. The 9 Skills (~22 min)

Each **deep** skill: definition · one frame · why now · 2 actions.
Each **light** skill: name · one line · one action · move.
The **medium-deep** skill sits between.

---

### 1. AI Literacy & Augmented Practice — DEEP (4 min)

- **Define:** AI literacy is understanding what these systems can do, what they can't, how they work, and where they break. Enough to know when to trust them, when to push them, and when to override them.
- **Why it matters:** Your engineers and PMs understand AI. If you're the only person in the room without working knowledge, *you're the person adding the least value.* Untenable for someone whose job is to advocate for the human.
- Can't build empathy for users being impacted by technology when we don't understand the technology; understanding users and the systems they work in is what we do best
- Literacy is what tells you what's actually possible, where the real constraints are, and where the technology can be pushed harder than the team assumes. That knowledge is the raw material now. Without it, you're accepting whatever you're handed. With it, you're the one saying "we can do better than this, and here's how."
- I worked on back office systems; didn't have real impact until I took the time to learn the tech
- You earn empathy and you earn credibility by understanding the material. And credibility is the whole game, because you can't influence what you don't understand well enough to have a real opinion about.
- Should light a fire under you that PMs and Eng are using AI now to do their jobs better and faster; if you're not doing the same, guess who is defining the experience? Not you.
- **Do this now:**
  1. Pick a tool and use it on a real problem. Not a demo, not a toy prompt. Something you actually have to solve. That's the only way this becomes real.
  2. Pick one voice in this space and start following them. You don't need ten. You need one you'll actually read. A few I'd point you to: Nate B. Jones, Allie Miller, Matt Paige, Lenny Rachitsky.

Start there. Reach for the next thing after that.

---

### 2. Change Management & Resilience — DEEP (4 min)

- **Define:** The deliberate practice of navigating — and for leaders, driving — significant change through urgency, coalition, and momentum.
- **The frame:** Kotter's principles apply with uncomfortable precision. Three matter most right now: **create urgency · enlist a volunteer army · generate short-term wins.**
- **Why now:** Believing you can navigate the largest paradigm shift in tech history without intentional change management isn't optimism — *it's naivety.*
- **Do this now:**
  1. Name your own urgency in writing: what specifically will you lose if you don't move in the next 90 days?
  2. Find your volunteer army — one person already moving. Learn from them or elevate them.
  - **Leader bonus (if time):** set up one short-term win — evidence of *better work,* not just learning — you can point to in 30 days.

---

### 3. Cross-Functional Influence — LIGHT (1 min)

- **Define:** Leading with a human-centered POV. Ethos · pathos · logos.
- **The line that matters:** *Execution without a human-centered POV is exactly what AI now does faster and cheaper.*
- **Do this now:** Before your next cross-functional meeting, write your POV in three sentences: what the human needs, what the business needs, what the tech makes possible.

---

### 4. Craft Mastery & Human-Centered Principles — LIGHT (1 min)

- **Define:** Relentless attention to the details that separate adequate from genuinely good.
- **The line that matters:** *In a world where AI approximates expertise cheaply, craft and intention are what separate a practitioner from an algorithm.*
- **Do this now:** Audit one recent piece of your work against Nielsen's heuristics or Rams' principles. Be honest about the gaps.

---

### 5. Systems Thinking — DEEP (4 min)

- **Define:** Seeing the whole system a human moves through and identifying where they get stuck — across teams, surfaces, functions.
- **The frame:** Humans don't care about your org chart. When the product feels fragmented, they don't blame the team that owns the surface. *They blame the brand.*
- **Why now:** AI is being bolted onto existing products by teams optimizing their own surface. The human is walking through a system nobody owns end-to-end. That's where the highest-impact work lives.
- **The reframe (carry this out of the room):** When you operate at the system level, you become the steel thread through every function's success. Their goals become your goals. Their success becomes your success.
- **Do this now:**
  1. Map the human journey across your product beyond the surface you own. Mark where handoffs break.
  2. Identify one adjacent team whose success depends on yours (or vice versa). Have a 30-min conversation about the shared human.

---

### 6. Problem Framing & Strategic Judgment — MEDIUM-DEEP (2.5 min)

- **Define:** Choosing the right problem to solve before choosing how to solve it.
- **The frame:** Roger Martin — the quality of the solution is determined by the quality of the problem definition. Drucker — *nothing is so useless as doing efficiently what shouldn't be done at all.*
- **Why now:** AI compresses the "how" to almost nothing. When execution gets cheap, the *framing* is the entire game.
- **Bridge from 5:** You just learned to see the whole system. Problem framing is how you pick which piece to work on first.
- **Do this now:** Before your next project kicks off, write the problem statement three ways. Pick the sharpest.

---

### 7. Product & Business Acumen — LIGHT (1 min)

- **Define:** Understanding how the business creates value and how your work connects it to the human.
- **The line that matters:** Drucker — *the purpose of business is to create a customer.* Understanding the business isn't a concession to corporate priorities. It's the most human-centered thing you can do.
- **Do this now:** This week, learn how your company actually makes money — unit economics, revenue model, top three metrics leadership tracks.

---

### 8. Data Literacy — LIGHT (1 min)

- **Define:** Fluency with evidence plus judgment to make rigor trade-offs and willingness to use data as a lever.
- **The line that matters:** Brené Brown — *stories are just data with a soul.* Data without human context is numbers. Human context without data is intuition. Great practitioners hold both.
- **Do this now:** Pick a decision that's stuck waiting for perfect data. Ship your best call based on what you know now. Document the trade-off.

---

### 9. Storytelling & Executive Communication — DEEP (4 min)

- **Define:** Telling the story of the human on the other side so clearly it travels — into prioritization, into how teams build, into how the company sells.
- **The frame:** *The work is not enough. It has never been enough.* A finding that stays in a deck. A design that never gets championed. None of it moves anything.
- **Why now:** AI forces decisions faster than any org can absorb. If you don't tell the human story, the void fills with someone else's — the features story, the pricing story, the brand story. The human on the other side experiences the gap between all three.
- **The reframe (carry this out of the room):** Great storytelling is scaffolding. It doesn't require the designer to be in the room. It requires the story to be good enough that everyone who *is* in the room can tell it.
- **Do this now:**
  1. Rewrite your last research readout or design presentation into a 3-sentence story a stranger could retell.
  2. Find one place to tell the human story outside your own function — a sales all-hands, a marketing sync, an engineering standup.

---

## IV. Return to Mastery — deeper (3 min)

- The trick: you don't build these serially. You build the posture that makes all nine possible.
- Mastery isn't the tenth item. It's the *condition.*
- Transactional practice is not good enough right now. The work shapes the relationship humans have with technology. That demands a real, ongoing relationship with the work itself.
- *Without that posture, everything I just described stays theoretical.*

## V. Close — Urgency + one action (2 min)

- Where you're strong, go deeper. Where you have gaps, close them. Not because someone asked. Because the moment demands it and the human on the other side deserves it.
- **The ask:** pick *one* action from today. Not five. One. Do it this week.
- Close on the article's line: *"the most important and interesting work in the world. And it has never been more available to the people who show up equipped to do it."*

## VI. Q&A (15 min)

## Design notes for delivery

- **The rhythm:** Deep · Deep · Light · Light · Deep · Medium · Light · Light · Deep. Deliver the lights fast and confident. If you slow down there, the room notices.
- **Don't apologize for going short.** No "I could spend a whole hour on this…" The confidence of a short section is what makes it land.
- **Bridge 5 → 6 out loud.** "You just learned to see the whole system. Now let's talk about how to pick the right piece of it to work on."
- **Bridge 8 → 9 out loud.** Brené Brown twice as a callback: "Brené Brown again, because she nailed something else worth carrying…"
- **Leader bonuses on 1 and 2 are optional.** Delivered if pacing allows. Cutting them is your first pressure valve if the deep skills run long.
- **First cut if you're still over time:** trim IV (Mastery deeper) to 2 min by focusing only on "mastery is the condition, not the tenth skill." That's the sentence that matters.
