## Draft (Tam)

It was 1976, I was twelve and lived in the lower class section of a small town. Every summer night, I met up with my best friend Jen and the neighborhood boys, Sovine, Hume, the Eikman brothers, Joel, Adam, Fred, to play nighttime kick the can.

The rules were simple. The can was placed square in the middle of the intersection of four roads. Two people were “it” and counted to 100 while the rest hid. Hiding could be done in any front or back yard for a half block in each direction. The “it” pair could have someone remain close to the can but they couldn’t just stand there and had to walk at least 100 feet away throughout the game. Once the “it” pair caught all of the hiders, the first two caught became the new “it” pair. If someone kicked the can, all of those caught were free to hide again.

The neighbors didn’t seem to mind us. Probably because most of them knew us all. We had one night where one of the ladies saw one of the boys on the front porch and came out and loudly asked him if she could help him. He was promptly caught by the “it” pair. One of the pair saw him and ran to jump over the can.

My favorite way to hide was to find a dark shadow at one of the corner houses where I could see the “it” pair and calculate the exact right time to run and kick the can. Even though Jen and I were the youngest of the crew, I was the champion kicker and was rarely caught.

Years later, I’ve lost track of all of my kick the can friends. Sovine passed away a few years back. Jen was at my Dad’s funeral a couple of years ago and we said “hi, how are you?”

I loved that summer.  If I could, I’d go play kick the can right now.

## Fbomb feedback (structure / strengthen — no rewrite)

### What’s working
- Rules are clear and playable.
- Porch lady is a perfect comic beat.
- Shadow-watcher / champion kicker is the heat.
- Closing wish to play again is clean.

### Structure
1. Open in the game sooner — age + lower-class town can wait one beat; can in the intersection is the hook.
2. Keep rules, but trim anything a night of play wouldn’t need. Protect the “it” pair walking 100 feet. “Long string of night time kick the can” can go; the scenes show the string.
3. Mid: porch catch → hide/kick craft → youngest but rarely caught.
4. Then the loss beat (Sovine / Jen at the funeral). One quiet hinge (“years later”) before it so it isn’t abrupt.
5. Close on transition + “I’d go play right now.” Tomboy→girly competes with the can — either tie it to those nights with one concrete detail, or save for another piece.

### Characters
Jen, Sovine, Hume, Eikmans are named — good. Give each one job in the night if possible (who was “it,” who hid bold, who lived where) so they aren’t only a list. Hume can stay light unless the apartments wink is wanted.

### Tone / strengthen
- “Amazingly accommodating” tells; the porch lady shows — cut the tell.
- Funeral “hi, how are you?” is devastating in its smallness — don’t explain it.
- “Time of transition” is abstract next to the can; trust the wish to play again.

### One risk
Sovine’s death + Dad’s funeral + identity shift is a lot of weight after a playful game. Two of those three may be enough for this short piece; three can tip warm into elegy. Pick what the reader should leave with.

### Spine check
Night game → cast in action → time passed → she’d still play. That holds.
