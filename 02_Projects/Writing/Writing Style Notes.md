# Writing Style Notes

Observed from Tam's edits to drafts (started with "BE 2.0.md"). Living document — update as more edits come in.

## Patterns seen so far

- **Full sentences over fragments.** I tend to write short punchy fragments for emphasis ("Curious about everything, decent at a lot of things..."). Tam rewrites these as complete sentences with a subject ("I'm curious about most things, decent at a lot of things...").
- **Cuts rhetorical flourish / wordplay.** Lines like "a cost, not just a charm" or tricolon endings ("even the things you're good at, even the things that pay") get replaced with plainer, more direct statements.
- **Grounds abstraction in concrete personal/professional detail.** Prefers tying a concept to something specific and real ("particularly as a leader of a large organization") over a poetic or abstract closing line.
- **Precise with terminology.** Corrects informal/common phrasing to the source's actual term (e.g. "hedgehog principle" → "hedgehog concept" once confirmed that's Collins's actual wording).
- **Open to data/specifics from the source material** — at one point added a research stat (avg. years to clarity/breakthrough in the Good to Great research) directly into a paragraph, though later cut it. Worth offering factual specifics as an option, not assuming they'll stay.
- **Keeps my structural bones** (three-circle framing, fox/hedgehog contrast, opening definition sentence) largely intact — edits are about tone and diction, not structure.

## Working takeaway for drafts

Write complete sentences, plain and direct, anchored in a concrete detail from Tam's actual life/work rather than a clever abstract turn of phrase. Save any wordplay-y flourish for a first pass only — expect it to get cut.
