My career has been the best series of happy accidents. Somehow, I’ve managed to turn something that I never thought I wanted to do into a nearly 4 decade adventure, education, and life affirming experience. 

It probably all started when your dad brought home an old school Macintosh computer. I must have been about 17 and the year would have been 1981 or 1982. We were the only people I knew with a computer. I made good use of it during college, mostly typing papers for my now wife who was horrified to use a computer. 

My first really job was working for Exxon Mobile at a project they had in LeBarge, Wyoming. The town was small, not a stop light in sight. My girlfriend (now wife) and I wanted to get away from our families for the summer and found a job up there. We lived in an old school house with a family who had renovated it. We had some wild experiences there, including being chased through the house by a big buck sheep but that is a story for another day. My first day on the job my boss came in carrying a big PC, one of the old ones that worked with dual floppy discs. “You know how to use one of these, right?” Of course I said yes! He gave me a pirated copy of a spreadsheet software and told me that he’d be bringing in a leader for me to get entered. Fortunately I found someone in the office who actually did know how to use the PC and the software and got a quick tutorial and some occasional help desk support from her and somehow managed to do my job very well. 

My actual career in tech started a few years later. I was a senior supervisor, running the phone center for a political and market research firm. It felt like an important job because I was in charge of over 400 people, setting schedules, making sure we were hitting our quotas.
