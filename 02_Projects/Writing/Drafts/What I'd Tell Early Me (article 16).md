Dear 25 year old me. Sure, you like that senior phone center supervisor role you're in because you get to be in charge of a lot of people and you have a lot of fun with friends you've made over the years. This has been a good job. Better than your time at Exxon in LeBarge, WY. Better than that one day at the rest home (that was a doozy!) Better than working in the mail room at DHI. When your sister comes to you and suggests you should apply for a job at WordPerfect, a local software company that is growing fast, say yes. Trust her when she says that all you have to know in the job interview is what autoexec.bat and config.sys do and don't forget the answer she tells you to give. When you get the job, you'll be scare and excited. You've only ever used your Dad's early generation Mac and now you'll be answering customer support questions from people using a word processor running on Microsoft DOS computers. It's OK. You're going to learn everything you need to know and even though you don't think it is possible, you're going to end up loving it. 

Today I write this to you looking back on a 38 year career in tech. Know there will be a handful of tough times but, for the most part, it is goin to be such a ride. Sure, you've dropped out of college, not once but twice, but what you've learned in your career is worth three lifetimes of college. Through it all there are 7 key life and career lessons you'll learn.

Stay humble and grateful for the opportunity.

Be hungry to learn and embrace every chance to do something new and different.

Say yes as often as you can but also learn how to say no every once in a while.

Embrace change and move forward with grace while also advocating for what you believe is best.

Be authentic; it will take you a while to get that but once you do, it pays dividends.

Take care of yourself and people around you. 

Remember to have fun.
