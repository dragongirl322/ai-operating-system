I spent most of my life involved in one sport or another yet I could never embrace running. It always felt so tedious and arduous to me but I did it frequently as a "should" to keep myself fit and lean. That all changed for me nearly 20 years ago when I decided that running would be a good hobby for me and set a goal of running a half marathon. I learned that running not only made me feel great physically but it unlocked a level of creativity and zest for life I didn't know I had in me. I got hooked. One of the things I loved about running is that I knew it would be hard, especially the first mile as my body acclimated to the physical stress, but I also knew exactly what the goal was and why and consistently felt the reward at the end of a long run. 

I've been in tech for almost 40 years and have spent nearly 20 years as a people leader and the past 10 at the executive leadership level. Throughout those years I have mostly loved the work and have mostly understood exactly what I was working toward and why. For the past year, I've been chasing something that was never entirely clear to me with a sense of urgency that was unrelenting. Some of that was created by the uncertainty the emergence of AI has created. Some of that was created by the ever changing landscape of the company I work for. Ultimately, it was all created by me and my desire to always be achieving, proving to myself that I'm good enough and maybe even great once in a while.
