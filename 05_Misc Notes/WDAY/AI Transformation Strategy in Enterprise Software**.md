

## **Why Workflow Depth Beats Interaction Volume**

## **Executive Summary**

Many enterprise software companies are currently making a critical strategic mistake in their AI transformation efforts:

They are optimizing for **AI interaction volume** rather than **enterprise value creation**.

This often leads organizations to focus heavily on casual-user experiences because those experiences generate the highest number of interactions. However, high interaction counts do not necessarily translate into meaningful customer value, platform dependence, expansion revenue, or long-term competitive advantage.

The strongest long-term AI opportunity in enterprise software is likely not maximizing low-value interactions for casual users. Instead, it is increasing the value density of AI within the workflows of professional and backstage users—the people whose effectiveness directly determines operational success.

---

# **Three Core Enterprise User Types**

## **1. Casual Users**

### **Examples**

- Employees
    
- Managers
    
- Occasional approvers
    

### **Typical Jobs-to-be-Done**

- Requesting time off
    
- Selecting benefits
    
- Submitting expenses
    
- Updating goals
    
- Administrative approvals
    

### **Characteristics**

- Large population size
    
- Low frequency of use for many workflows
    
- Software is not central to their core job success
    
- Interactions are often company-mandated
    

### **AI Value Type**

- Convenience
    
- Reduced friction
    
- Faster completion
    
- Simplified navigation
    

### **Strategic Risk**

AI investment here often creates:

- high interaction volume
    
- low economic value density
    
- weak ROI justification

# **The Core Strategic Mistake: Optimizing for Volume**

Consumption-based pricing models create a dangerous incentive structure.

The temptation becomes:

- maximize AI actions
    
- increase interaction frequency
    
- optimize for engagement metrics
    
- prioritize broad usage surfaces
    

This often pushes companies toward casual-user AI experiences because they naturally generate large volumes of interactions.

However, enterprise customers do not primarily buy software based on interaction frequency.

They buy based on:

- operational efficiency
    
- risk reduction
    
- labor leverage
    
- compliance
    
- business continuity
    
- throughput
    
- decision quality
    
- revenue enablement
    
- organizational scalability
    

A benefits-selection AI interaction may happen millions of times.

But if it only saves users 90 seconds twice per year, customers may not perceive enough value to justify meaningful AI expansion spending.

Meanwhile:

- reducing payroll processing time by 30%
    
- accelerating recruiting throughput
    
- improving financial forecasting accuracy
    
- reducing implementation complexity
    

…can create massive operational and financial value.

# **Why Casual-User-Only AI Strategies Often Fail**

A casual-user-first strategy can create several problems:

## **1. Low Value Density**

The value per interaction is often small:

- reduced clicks
    
- easier navigation
    
- faster completion
    

These improvements matter, but they rarely transform business performance.

---

## **2. Weak Economic Justification**

Customers may question:

“Why are we paying significant AI consumption costs for convenience features?”

This creates pricing resistance.

---

## **3. Weak Platform Dependence**

Casual-user workflows:

- are often infrequent
    
- have lower cognitive complexity
    
- are easier to replace
    
- generate weaker operational dependence
    

This limits expansion potential and competitive moat creation.

---

## **4. Adoption Ceiling**

If AI value remains shallow:

- customers limit rollout
    
- governance becomes restrictive
    
- finance organizations push back on spend
    
- usage plateaus
    

---

# **The Strategic Importance of Backstage Users**

Backstage users may ultimately create the largest long-term strategic advantage.

These users determine:

- implementation speed
    
- platform extensibility
    
- governance quality
    
- scalability
    
- interoperability
    
- downstream experience quality
    

AI investments here can:

- dramatically reduce deployment complexity
    
- accelerate customer time-to-value
    
- improve ecosystem expansion
    
- increase platform stickiness
    
- create durable competitive differentiation
    

In enterprise software, backstage leverage often compounds more powerfully than front-stage interaction volume.

- Decision acceleration

If AI consistently improves the work of:

- recruiters
    
- analysts
    
- payroll specialists
    
- planners
    
- administrators
    

…those users become:

- advocates
    
- organizational influencers
    
- rollout accelerators
    
- trusted evaluators of AI effectiveness
    

Casual users rarely drive enterprise platform expansion decisions.

Operational leaders do.

---

# **The Strategic Conclusion**

The companies most likely to win enterprise AI will probably not be the companies generating the highest number of AI interactions.

They will be the companies whose AI becomes indispensable to:

- operational workflows
    
- organizational throughput
    
- enterprise decision-making
    
- system configuration
    
- business scalability
    

The future competitive moat is not interaction volume.

It is operational dependence.

And operational dependence is most often created through:

1. professional-user workflow transformation
    
2. backstage-user platform leverage
    
3. trust earned through measurable business outcomes
    

Not through maximizing low-value casual-user interactions.
