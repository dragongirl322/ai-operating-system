Ultimately ran 3 studies focused on helping us understand attributes of delightful photos; initial observations that led to this:

- In early EMG studies being run to pilot technology and develop protocol, we noticed most frequent cause of delight was photos—hotel photos, destination photos, food photos, etc.
    
- Around that same time running field studies to understand family travel shoppers and their decision making process with finding and booking hotel; noted instances where people made a choice strongly driven by photo—alligator slide, balcony—so photos not primary decision driver but effective at breaking the tie and inspiring confidence

- Study 1 = 17 participants in lab, on our site
    
- Study 2=15 participants in lab, on whatever site they chose, then evaluate a pre-selected set of photos representing a broad range for search results and hotel details pages
    
- Study 3=Survey using conjoint analysis w/ N=246 evaluating a defined set of SR and Detail pages

Summary of final study:

Based on the two in-depth EMG studies conducted previously, there were four categories of photos we wanted to study for hotel search results and hotel detail pages. For hotel search results, we set out to determine which category of photo would elicit most positive response and attributes of each types of photos that improve changes of positive response. For hotel detail pages, the goal was to identify attributes of photos that are most likely to elicit a positive emotions for travel shoppers. The final study was an online survey, designed by the late Dr. Richard Hazlett (he sadly passed away last year) and administered by Merrill Research, Inc. A total of 246 travel shoppers completed the survey.

  

Search Results Photos

The four categories of photos evaluated are:

1. Outside building
    
2. Building entrance
    
3. Lobby/common area
    
4. Guestroom
    

  

Respondents were shown 24 groups consisting of 4 photos in each group. Photos were size that appears on hotel search results. They were asked to select the one they found most inviting and the one they found least inviting from the 4 photos. By design, photos showed up in a variety of groups to allow for comparisons for each photo against different groups of photos. 

  

The most inviting photo category is Guestroom, chosen by 90% of respondents. The second most inviting category is lobby, chosen by 10% of respondents. The least inviting category is building chosen by 82% of respondents as least inviting.

  

Hotel detail page Photos

  

For Hotel detail page photos, we focused exclusively on guestroom photos as the previous two studies had shown us these were the photos that created the frequent emotional responses. The four categories of photos studied are:

1. Window vs. no window
    
2. Vista out window vs. just lighted by window 
    
3. Distorted fish eye quality vs. normal lens photo
    
4. Sitting area clearly shown vs. not
    

  

  

Respondents were shown 17 groups consisting of 4 photos in each group at same size as photos on hotel detail pages. They were asked to select the one they found most inviting and the one they found least inviting from the 4 photos. By design, photos showed up in a variety of groups to allow for comparisons for each photo against different groups of photos. 

  

**Window or No Window**

When room images were directly compared on the presence of a window, the room with a window was the significant choice for ‘most inviting’ two-thirds of the time.  However, these results indicate that it was still possible to create delight in the shopper without a window, for in one-third of these comparisons a no-window image was the significant choice for most inviting. When these delightful no-window images are analyzed, it is found that they all created an atmosphere with lighting, or comfort and luxury that went beyond most guestroom images.

**Vista out window vs. just lighted by window**

In these comparisons having a clear view of the cityscape was significant 60% of the time as most inviting, and having the room lit by natural light, without a clear view of the cityscape, was significant 40% of the time as most inviting. This finding shows that a clear view of the cityscape was not the all important element, and a window lighting the room with natural light was a significant source of delight unto itself.

**Sitting Area clearly shown**

When a sitting area was clearly shown in the guestroom image, it was the significant choice for ‘most inviting’ 88% of the time. In fact, in only one of these eight comparisons was a guest room without a sitting area the significant choice for ‘most inviting’.  A sense of spaciousness and a place to sit and enjoy the room and view besides the bed was an important element in the image for hotel shoppers.

  

**Distortions in Images**

In 80% of these comparisons, the image with a notable fisheye distortion was chosen as the least inviting guestroom image, duplicating the findings from the previous two studies, run in the lab using EMG, on the detrimental effects of image distortion.
