Polanyi’s revenge” is a shorthand for the idea that tacit knowledge matters: when organizations try to formalize and automate work, the unarticulated, context-sensitive parts can come back as failures, edge cases, and unintended consequences.

### Why it matters now

As AI systems are trained on documented artifacts and explicit rules, the real-world success still depends on nuance: context, intent, norms, and judgment. When tacit knowledge is missing, automation can scale the wrong thing.

### Implications for design

- Documenting is not the same as understanding; artifacts may omit the crucial “why.”
- Interfaces that appear deterministic can hide uncertainty and degrade trust.
- Designers may need to surface assumptions, ambiguity, and escalation paths.
- The role of human judgment may become more valuable, not less.

### Questions I’m exploring

- What tacit knowledge is essential in my domain, and how can it be supported without pretending it’s fully codifiable?
- What are patterns for designing with uncertainty, exceptions, and power dynamics?
- How do we design systems that respect expertise while still enabling scale?
