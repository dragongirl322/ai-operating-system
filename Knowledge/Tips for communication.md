**CONTEXT:** What people hear is more important than what you say. Use these tips to ensure that your audience hears you. This is even more critical as the audiences grow, are not in the same time zone, do not share a native language, and more.

These tips apply to any type of communication (verbal, written, and presentations).

  

**PREPARATION**

Answer before starting. Then choose the communication medium that is appropriate. There is not a strict formula for “if this, then that”. But answering these two questions will force you to think about the medium.

**1. What is the goal?**

The most common:

a) FYI - audience needs to know. Example: Sales FYI about product availability

b) Approval - of a proposal. Example: Marketing content for a Boost event.

c) Follow up - audience asked for specific information. Example: Details about our outsourced service budgets by region.

d) Inspiration - make the audience remember your point. Example: Orientation talk. Sales Conference main stage. Recruiting presentation

  

**2. Who is the audience?**

a) Number of attendees?

b) Format? (Written or presented? live or asynchronous? video conference or in person? big room or at a table?)

c) Are they familiar with the topic?

**TIPS FOR ANY TYPE OF COMMUNICATION (with examples!)**

**1. Less is more**

Communicate just enough to achieve your goal.

Your brevity is more impressive (and useful) than additional facts.

Example: Messenger length (vs. Email length). Email (vs. attachments). Pictures (vs. words)

Why: your audience is unlikely to read / hear everything anyway

  

**2. Use headlines**

Good Headline: "Custom audience adoption reached 1M advertisers, growing 20% in the last month"

Bad headline: "Chart of Custom Audience Adoption in Q2"

Why: Ensure audience knows your key point

  

**3. Graph data**

Graphs: Up-and-to-the-right; a waterfall chart, a heat map

NOT graphs: clip art

Why: Easy-to-understand, concise, and enable your audience to SEE the answer

  

**4. Enhance with stories**

Story: "Hawkers (Spanish SMB) now spends 1M euros on ads. They started at 1 euro. They're a good example of . . . "

Story: "Here are screenshots of the first three SMB ads on Instagram showing . . . "

NOT Story: "SMBs from around the world are growing fast by using Facebook"

Why: They're memorable and compliment (not replace) the data.

  

**5. Show 'significant' digits**

Significant: $1.2M or ~$1M

NOT significant: $1,234,567.98 or $1,000,000.00

Why: Does not imply false precision. Also, see tip #1.

  

**6. Use ranges**

Range: Revenue lift is between 5 and 15%. OR Revenue lift is 10% +/- 5%. Or in error bars

NOT range: revenue is up 5.34%

Why: Does not imply false precision.

  

**7. Make graphs readable**

(Examples needed - visual)

Fill the page (vs. big borders)

Single dark line (vs. multi line, dashed, or multi-color)

Two dimension (vs. three dimensional)

Two axis (vs. three axis)

Clear labels

Why: The data can't tell the story if the audience can't see it.

  

**8. Avoid acronyms**

Exceptions: SMB, FB, FYI, regions

Why: Avoids potential confusion, especially for new team members.

  

**9. Pre-read**

Send before meeting

Why: Helps focus the time for discussion (what the audience can ONLY do in a meeting)

  

**PRESENTATIONS (live audience)**

**1. Visual material**

Otherwise, use one-page written format (docs, email, etc.)

  

**2. Animation**

Effective with live, large group presentations

Not for printed materials

  

**WRITTEN COMMUNICATION (email / group posts)**

**HEADLINE: You have a 'three second audition'**

Much like newsfeed, many people read email or presentations or reports quickly. If your goal is to write a lot, then do what you're doing. If your goal is to make the audience understand or care or take action, adapt your communication to how they comprehend information.

  

**1. Say what you want**

Calls to action: "FYI:" "Action needed: (specifics)" "Approval needed: (specifics)" etc.

For email - make it the subject

For group posts - make it the first line

For attached presentations - summarize it

Examples (good):

- FYI: GeoProfile currency logic change grows games payers by 2%"

**-** Changes to India SMB Team: I wanted to let you know about 3 changes with the SMB team in India.

Examples (bad):

- Can someone please tell me who is responsible for this

- After-action report (AAR) posted for NA case study email outreach. There were some interesting findings. Review it [here](https://www.facebook.com/groups/541268192588467/).

- Gday Everyone, as you might know tomorrow in the Sydney office we are hosting a day workshop for the first ever [Facebook Small Business Accelerator](https://www.facebook.com/smallbizaccelerator) .

  

**2. Bullets (or numbered lists)** are better than **sentences** are better than **paragraphs**

  

  

**QUESTIONS I FREQUENTLY ASK IN MEETINGS**

1. What is your goal (overall or for this communication)?
    
2. How will you know if you're successful? The answer should be numeric/measurable
    
3. What is the magnitude? I'm seeking to understand the basic size: dozens? thousands? millions?
    
4. Who is responsible? (for the next step? for a deliverable?)
    
5. When can I expect the next update/follow-up?
    
6. What would you need to go twice as fast? I'm looking to see if you understand the constraints.
    
7. What would you stop doing to work on this instead? I'm seeking to understand your prioritization
